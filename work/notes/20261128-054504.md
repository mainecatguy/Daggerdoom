# Townspeople who walk and use buildings: architecture proposal (run 20261128-054504)

Research only; no game code changed. Sources: code read on this branch, notes `20261123-215225-townspeople.md`,
`20261124-004803.md` (at home).

## 0. Main finding: walking already exists

The persistent townspeople built on 2026-11-23 already walk. `Townsfolk.cs` gives every person a daily schedule
(Worker, Homebody, Roamer, NightOwl, Pacer, Guard habits; home, haunts, wake/sleep). Each step is *inside* (hidden),
*outside* (stand, or pace between two nearby spots) or *walking* (A* over Daggerfall's city nav grid between two
buildings). `DoomerfallTownspeople.cs` sends the nearest 128 visible people every 0.5 s as position + velocity
(`vx * WorldTime.TimeScale`, 0 when paused); `dd_townsfolk.cpp` dead-reckons them between lists with `SetOrigin`
and plays the walk sprite. The 2026-11-23 run measured walkers moving (`fa82cc0b32daa2e3` 1850,-13312 to
1836,-13281 over 3 s). So the owner's "they don't move" is probably one of:

- **Most people visible at a moment are not walking.** An outside step stands still 35% of the time; pacers pause
  20-120 game s (2-10 real s) at each end. Walks between buildings are rare: the 2026-11-23 census found 906 of
  1033 awake samples at home or a haunt, so only about 12% were walking between buildings.
- **Speed.** 5 DF units/game s = 45 Doom units/real s, a real walking pace, but slow next to a Doom player (~300+).
- **At night** everyone is home and stands (kept by the owner).
- **A regression** since 2026-11-24 (not run here; it needs a fresh build of both engines). The first card below
  measures it.

What is really missing: people vanish at a spot *near* a building instead of going through its door; inside
buildings only residents at home are shown (no shopkeepers' customers, tavern visitors, arrivals or departures);
walkers ignore Doom geometry and actors; nobody reacts to anything.

## 1. What Daggerfall Unity has

- **Vanilla** `PopulationManager`: a pool of 24 mobiles per 16 RMB blocks (max 96) spawned within 96 units of the
  player, recycled past 150 m, by day only. `MobilePersonMotor` random-walks them on `CityNavigation`. No schedules,
  no homes, no identity: a mobile is forgotten when recycled. One in 32 is a guard.
- **Doomerfall's roster** (above) replaces that for bridged games: up to 1200 people per town, ids from map id +
  index, positions a pure function of (seed, world time). The only saved state is the ledger: deaths and corpses.
- **Who depends on a townsperson's position.** Quests: none. `Game/Questing` never references `MobilePersonNPC` or
  `PopulationManager`; quest people are `StaticNPC`s at markers. Talk (`DoomerfallTalk`) finds the person by id and
  uses distance only. Crime: vanilla `PlayerEntity.SpawnCityGuards` turns nearby guard mobiles from
  `PopulationPool` into city-watch knights; Doom guards are handled by `dd_guards.cpp` and todo card `outdoorguards`.
  Debug: todo card `findnpc` points at a person, which needs DFU's computed pose to stay the truth.

## 2. Who owns what

- **Daggerfall:** the roster, schedules, homes, who is alive, corpses, and "where should this person be now". It
  stays a pure function of time, so off-screen people need no simulation and nobody vanishes (object permanence).
- **Doom:** the body in the live area: moving it, collision, sprites, damage, and any combat AI once it turns
  hostile.
- **Handoff.** Daggerfall sends *legs*, not frames: person id, from/to points (door or spot), the path, start and end
  game time, and the step after it. Doom walks the leg itself and is told only when the list changes (a leg starts,
  a step ends, a menu runs the clock). If Doom's body falls more than ~2 s behind the schedule (blocked), it snaps
  to the scheduled spot when out of the player's view. Today's 0.5 s position + velocity list is the degenerate form
  of this, and can stay as the fallback.
- **Doom reports back:** deaths (exists), and *detached* (the person switched to combat AI or fled). Daggerfall
  stops listing a detached person for this visit; on leaving the area they rejoin their schedule (or stay dead).
- **Saved:** nothing new for walkers. Daggerfall keeps the ledger; the Doom snapshot of the current map restores
  bodies exactly; a rebuild from the record re-derives everyone from time. A detached-but-alive person needs no
  save entry unless the owner wants grudges to persist (design call, belongs to `npc-self-defense-doom-bot`).

## 3. Walking in Doom

- **Paths.** Keep Daggerfall's city nav grid (64 DF units = 48 Doom units per cell; roads preferred). It matches
  the converted town because both come from the same RMB data. Doom only follows waypoints, so UZDoom needs no
  pathfinding (`P_NewChaseDir` has none). Moving with `P_TryMove` instead of `SetOrigin` stops walking through
  Doom-only things (door objects, props); a blocked walker waits, then side-steps, then snaps out of view.
- **Doors.** Today a building's site is the walkable cell nearest its centre (`TownRoster` ctor, search radius 24
  cells), so people vanish in the street. Daggerfall's `StaticDoor` (`buildingKey`, `centre`, `normal`) gives each
  building's real street door. Ending and starting walk legs there makes people visibly walk in and out. Interiors
  are separate Doom maps, so nobody crosses maps: going in is "reach the door, disappear"; coming out is "appear in
  the doorway, walk off".
- **Inside.** Extend the at-home list to "everyone whose step is *inside* this building now": workers at their
  workplace, tavern visitors, shop customers. A person whose walk ends here appears at the interior entry marker at
  arrival; one leaving walks to it and disappears. Indoor wandering: short moves between Rest/Enter markers and the
  spots the at-home feature already picks with DFU's interior colliders.
- **Crowds.** Daggerfall lists at most 128 rows; a walker is a passive actor that blocks nothing and is never
  targeted, costing one move and floor check per tic. That is far below a slaughter pack (hundreds of monsters with
  AI and LOS checks). DFU's cost is the 1200-person scan every 0.5 s with cached A* paths, measured cheap in the
  original run. Risk only if the 128 cap is raised a lot.
- **Guards and hostile townspeople.** Guards keep their patrol loop as walkers until a crime; then `outdoorguards`
  bots take over. A townsperson who turns hostile switches to the one Doom bot template (`npc-self-defense`) and
  is reported detached. Both stay out of this work.

## 4. Risks, options, recommendation

| Option | What | Cost | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Keep the puppet | DFU poses every 0.5 s, Doom dead-reckons; add doors and inside-building lists | small, mostly C# | walk through Doom props; no reaction possible |
| B. Leg handoff (recommended) | A, plus Doom walks each leg with `P_TryMove` and reports detached | medium: bridge event + ~200 lines in `dd_townsfolk.cpp` | drift from schedule; needs the snap rule |
| C. Doom owns schedules | Doom runs the day plan | large | breaks "Daggerfall owns permanent state"; rejected |

Other risks: the protocol needs a leg event (version bump); doors blocked by converter-placed objects; menus that
run the clock must re-send legs (`PublishNow` already exists); `findnpc` must read DFU's pose, not Doom's.

**Recommended first card (one card, Daggerfall side only, no protocol change):** *Townspeople use real doors.*
(1) Measure on a fresh build that day walkers move in Gothway (`tools/townspeople` harness: walkers' displacement
over 10 s, share of listed people with non-zero velocity). Fix it if it regressed. (2) Anchor each building's site
at its `StaticDoor` (door centre pushed ~1 cell out along `normal`, snapped to the nav grid). Walk legs end and
start there, so people visibly go in and come out. Check: in Gothway at 8:00 and 18:00, people disappear within
one cell of a door and none in open street. Then, as separate cards: inside-building occupants (shops, taverns,
arrivals and departures), then option B's leg handoff with collision.
