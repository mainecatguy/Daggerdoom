# Townspeople who walk and use buildings: architecture proposal (run 20261128-054504)

Research only; no game code changed. Sources: code read on this branch (`Townsfolk.cs`, `DoomerfallTownspeople.cs`,
`dd_townsfolk.cpp`), notes `20261123-215225-townspeople.md` and `20261124-004803.md` (at home). Revised after a
verifier review: the vanishing mechanisms, night walkers, the load behavior, and the census claim are corrected.

## 0. Main finding: walking already exists

The persistent townspeople built on 2026-11-23 already walk. `Townsfolk.cs` gives every person a daily schedule
(Worker, Homebody, Roamer, NightOwl, Pacer, Guard habits; home, haunts, wake/sleep). Each step is *inside* (hidden),
*outside* (stand, or pace to a cell up to 6 cells away and back), or *walking* (A* over Daggerfall's city nav grid
between two buildings). `DoomerfallTownspeople.cs` sends the nearest 128 visible people every 0.5 s as position +
velocity (`vx * WorldTime.TimeScale`, 0 when paused). `dd_townsfolk.cpp` moves them between lists with `SetOrigin`
and plays the walk sprite. The 2026-11-23 run measured walkers moving (`fa82cc0b32daa2e3` 1850,-13312 to
1836,-13281 over 3 s). Possible reasons for "they don't move", **none measured on today's build**:

- **Many visible people stand.** An outside step stands still 35% of the time (`Pose`, `r.Value() < 0.35`). Every
  pacing outside step also pauses 20-120 game s (2-10 real s) at each end (`Townsfolk.cs:413`). The share of
  *visible* people moving at a given moment is unmeasured. The 2026-11-23 census (906 of 1033 awake samples at home
  or a haunt) counts everyone, including people hidden indoors and moving pacers, so it does not answer this.
- **Speed.** 5 DF units/game s = 45 Doom units/real s, a real walking pace, but slow next to a Doom player (~300+).
- **Night.** Most people are home and stand (kept by the owner). Night walkers are the exceptions: a third of guards
  work a 12 h night shift fully outdoors (`Townsfolk.cs:218-221`). NightOwls wake around 15:00 and stay up 10-13 h,
  mostly at taverns. Pacers are awake 17-21 h a day, 95% outdoors.
- **A regression** since 2026-11-24 (not run here; that needs a fresh build of both engines). The first card checks.

**Why people pop in and out of sight, with no door** (two mechanisms):

1. **Walk ends are personal anchors, not the building.** A site is the walkable cell nearest the building centre
   (`TownRoster` constructor, searched up to 24 cells away). Each person's walk ends at `Anchor(p, site)`
   (`Townsfolk.cs:480-496`), a random cell up to ±3 cells from the site, plus ±20 units.
2. **Stays flip inside/outside in place.** `BuildPlan` (`Townsfolk.cs:338-343`) cuts each stay into 0.4-1.4 h chunks,
   each randomly inside (kind 0) or outside (kind 1). When an outside chunk ends, the person vanishes wherever their
   pacing has taken them (up to 6 cells from the anchor, `Wander`) and later reappears at the anchor. They do not
   walk to the building.

Also missing: inside buildings only residents at home are shown (no customers, tavern visitors, arrivals or
departures); walkers ignore Doom geometry and actors; nobody reacts to anything.

## 1. What Daggerfall Unity has

- **Vanilla** `PopulationManager`: 24 mobiles per 16 RMB blocks (max 96) spawned within 96 units of the player,
  recycled past 150 m, by day only. `MobilePersonMotor` random-walks them on `CityNavigation`. No schedules, homes or
  identity: a mobile is forgotten when recycled. One in 32 is a guard.
- **Doomerfall's roster** replaces that in bridged games: up to 1200 people per town, ids from map id + index,
  positions a pure function of (seed, world time). The only saved state is the ledger: deaths and corpses.
- **Who depends on a townsperson's position.** Quests: none. `Game/Questing` never references `MobilePersonNPC` or
  `PopulationManager`; quest people are `StaticNPC`s at markers. Talk (`DoomerfallTalk`) finds the person by id and
  uses distance only. Crime: vanilla `PlayerEntity.SpawnCityGuards` turns nearby guard mobiles from
  `PopulationPool` into city-watch knights; Doom guards are `dd_guards.cpp` and todo card `outdoorguards`. Debug:
  todo card `findnpc` points at a person, so DFU's computed pose must stay the truth.

## 2. Who owns what

- **Daggerfall:** the roster, schedules, homes, who is alive, corpses, and where each person should be now. That
  stays a pure function of time, so off-screen people need no simulation and nobody vanishes (object permanence).
- **Doom:** the body in the live area: moving it, collision, sprites, damage, and combat AI once it turns hostile.
- **Handoff.** Daggerfall sends *legs*, not frames: id, from/to points (door or spot), the path, start/end game time.
  Doom walks the leg itself and gets a new list only when something changes (a leg starts, a step ends, a menu runs
  the clock). A body more than ~2 s behind schedule (blocked) snaps to the scheduled spot out of the player's view.
  Today's 0.5 s position + velocity list is the simplest form of this and stays as the fallback.
- **Doom reports back:** deaths (exists), and *detached* (switched to combat AI or fled). Daggerfall stops listing
  a detached person this visit; after leaving the area they rejoin their schedule (or stay dead).
- **Saved, as built today.** Daggerfall's ledger only. Doom's snapshot does **not** keep townspeople: after a load
  `DD_TownsfolkResync` (`dd_townsfolk.cpp:459-470`) clears all rows, `DD_TownsfolkTick` destroys every saved
  townsperson actor, and the next Daggerfall list respawns everyone at their scheduled positions.
- **Option B on load.** Walkers keep that behavior: a delay or a blocked walker is transient, and snapping to the
  schedule on load is correct. Detached people are different. They must become a separate Doom combat class that the
  resync does not destroy, so the snapshot keeps them. After a load Doom re-sends its detached ids, so Daggerfall
  does not list them a second time. Whether a grudge outlives the visit is a design call for `npc-self-defense`.

## 3. Walking in Doom

- **Paths.** Keep Daggerfall's city nav grid (64 DF units = 48 Doom units per cell; roads preferred). It matches the
  converted town because both come from the same RMB data. Doom only follows waypoints, so UZDoom needs no
  pathfinding. Moving with `P_TryMove` instead of `SetOrigin` stops walking through Doom-only things (door objects,
  props); a blocked walker waits, side-steps, then snaps out of view.
- **Doors.** Daggerfall's `StaticDoor` (`buildingKey`, `centre`, `normal`) gives each building's real street door.
  Both mechanisms in section 0 must go through it: walk legs between buildings start and end at the door, and every
  inside/outside flip inside a stay becomes a short walk (door to anchor when stepping out, current spot to anchor to
  door when going in). Pacing targets are already line-clear from the anchor (`Wander`/`Clear`), so that return
  path needs no new search. Interiors are separate Doom maps, so nobody crosses maps: going in is "reach the door,
  disappear", coming out is "appear in the doorway, walk off".
- **Inside.** Extend the at-home list to everyone whose step is *inside* this building now: workers at their
  workplace, tavern visitors, shop customers. Someone whose walk ends here appears at the interior entry marker on
  arrival; someone leaving walks to it and disappears. Indoor wandering: short moves between Rest/Enter markers and
  the spots the at-home feature already picks with DFU's interior colliders.
- **Night walkers.** Night guards, NightOwls and Pacers use the same doors and legs. NightOwls make taverns the
  busiest night interiors, so the inside list must hold more than residents for taverns to look right at night.
- **Crowds.** At most 128 listed rows; a walker is a passive actor that blocks nothing and is never targeted, so it
  costs one move and floor check per tic. That is far below a slaughter pack. DFU scans up to 1200 people every
  0.5 s with cached A* paths, measured cheap in the original run.
- **Guards and hostile townspeople.** Guards walk their patrol loop until a crime; then `outdoorguards` bots take
  over. A townsperson who turns hostile switches to the one Doom bot template (`npc-self-defense`) and is reported
  detached. Both stay out of this work.

## 4. Risks, options, recommendation

| Option | What | Cost | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Keep the puppet | DFU poses every 0.5 s, Doom dead-reckons; add doors and inside-building lists | small, mostly C# | walk through Doom props; no reaction possible |
| B. Leg handoff (recommended) | A, plus Doom walks each leg with `P_TryMove`, reports detached, keeps detached bodies on load | medium: bridge event + ~200 lines in `dd_townsfolk.cpp` | drift from schedule; needs the snap rule |
| C. Doom owns schedules | Doom runs the day plan | large | breaks "Daggerfall owns permanent state"; rejected |

Other risks: the protocol needs a leg event (version bump); doors blocked by converter-placed objects; menus that run
the clock must re-send legs (`PublishNow` exists); `findnpc` must read DFU's pose, not Doom's.

**Recommended first card (one card, `Townsfolk.cs` only, no protocol or Doom change):** *Townspeople use real doors.*
(1) On a fresh build, measure in Gothway at 12:00 the share of listed people with non-zero velocity, and walkers'
displacement over 10 s (`tools/townspeople` harness). If walking regressed, fix that first. (2) Put each building's
site at its `StaticDoor` (centre pushed ~1 cell out along `normal`, snapped to a walkable cell). (3) Walk legs end
and start at that door point, not at the personal anchor; the anchor is kept only as the standing/pacing spot.
(4) In `BuildPlan`/`Pose`, each switch between an inside and an outside chunk gets a short walk between door and
anchor (going in: from the pacing spot to the anchor, then to the door). (5) Check, at 8:00, 12:00 and 18:00:
every hide and every appearance of a listed person is within one cell of a building door, and none happens in open
street. Log the distance in the existing `-dftownreport` test hook. This stays within one card: the changes are
plan building and pose math in one file, and the harness already exists. Then, as separate cards: inside-building
occupants (shops, taverns, arrivals and departures), then option B's leg handoff with collision.

## Failed approaches / not done

No launch was made: the review builds are from 2026-11-24, and rebuilding both engines for a research note was not
worth it. The movement question is step (1) of the first card. The moving share among visible people was not
computed. It needs the roster built from game data, which means running Daggerfall Unity.
