# doorsolid: open doors are solid (run 20261124-202008)

## Plan

1. `door_objects.zs`: keep the door links solid in every pose. Stop a swinging door that would hit a body instead of
   letting it swing through. Never push anyone.
2. Update `door_check` and `action_regress.py` to the new rule without weakening the other checks.
3. Update the parity tool (`parity.py`, `Parity.cs`): open stages compare against solid open leaves.
4. Run everything on fresh conversions, then build the demo.

## What changed

- `tools/dungeon_converter/door_objects.zs` (dungeons, interiors, runtime dungeons and city gates all use it):
  - Links are solid when closed, opening, open, and closing.
  - **Swinging into a body:** before each step the door checks its next pose. If that pose would overlap a player or
    monster, the door waits where it is and keeps trying each tic, so it goes on as soon as the body moves. Pressing
    Use while it waits sends it back, as before. It never moves into a body, so it cannot push anyone or trap them
    against a wall. The body is free to walk away, because the door's current pose never overlaps it.
  - Ordinary doors wait only for bodies (players and monsters). They ignore solid props such as braziers, as before,
    so a prop in the swing cannot hold a door forever (relevant to the `flatcollision` todo). City gates still wait
    for any solid actor, unchanged.
  - **Safety:** a body can still end up inside a door's links without the door moving into it, for example when it is
    spawned or teleported there. Doom cannot push it out, and solid links would hold it fast. So each door checks for
    a body inside its links once a second (spread over the tics by position), and every tic while one is inside. The
    links stay non-solid until the body leaves. This replaces the old rule ("solid only when fully closed and clear").
  - There is no debug output. The only console line is the existing `DFDOORS spawned` line.
- `tools/dungeon_converter/door_check/zscript.zs`:
  - `solid` now expects all links solid in all six samples (closed, opening, open, closing, closed again).
  - `openpass` is now `openblock`. Walking into the open leaf's face must be stopped by the door, at no more than the
    leaf's half thickness plus the body radius, plus one 2-unit step.
  - `through` is now `block`. A body in the swing path must hold the opening door partway, with no overlap and the links
    solid. After the body leaves, the door must finish opening.
  - `hold` now means: the closing door waits on a body in the doorway (no overlap, solid). Once the body leaves, the
    door closes and is solid. Then a body spawned inside the closed door must make the links let go within 36 tics, and
    they must turn solid again once it is gone.
  - The real player and a real dormant Imp (`DTPLAYERSTALL`) now report `held+stopped+closed`.
  - New `wide=a/b` diagnostic, not scored: the widest body radius that walks through the open doorway with the solid
    leaf (a) and with the leaf non-solid (b).
- `tools/dungeon_converter/action_regress.py`: checks `openblock` and `block` instead of `openpass` and `through`. The
  body check expects `held+stopped+closed`. The door launch limit is 75 s, because the body checks now wait on both swing
  directions.
- `tools/dungeon_compare/parity.py`: the opened door stage must be swung *and solid*.
- `tools/dungeon_converter/Parity.cs` and `Program.cs`: in the open stages, Daggerfall's side now keeps each door's mesh,
  swung 90 degrees about its hinge exactly as `door_objects.zs` places it (reversed on flipped doors). Before, it removed
  the doors. `DoorInfo` gained `Instance` and `SwingDir`. The real-Unity flood validation still uses no doors, because
  that is what Daggerfall Unity does.

## Open question: open leaves shut stock-size monsters out of doorways (answered)

Owner answer (2026-11-24): narrow monsters on Daggerfall maps in the same proportion as the player. Done in
`work/notes/20261124-210422.md`: radius-20 monsters now pass 45 of 46 open doorways with solid leaves.

The doors in Privateer's Hold are 36 units long and 4.5 thick. The `wide=a/b/c` diagnostic in `door_check` reports the
widest body radius that walks through each open doorway: (a) a player body with the solid leaf, (b) a player body with
the leaf non-solid, (c) a monster body with the solid leaf. Results:

- 25 doorways: **18 / 24** with a solid or non-solid leaf
- 17 doorways: **15 / 18**
- 2 doorways: 16 / 18, 1 doorway: 11 / 11, and 12 dead ends with no room.

The player (10.5) fits everywhere. Most Doom monsters have radius 20 (Imp, Zombieman, DRLA former humans), and they
keep stock sizes on Daggerfall maps. Before this task they could pass 25 open doorways (the leaf was not solid) and were
already too wide for the other 17. With solid open leaves they fit through **no open doorway** in Privateer's Hold. The
unscored `monster=` field shows this directly: 26 doorways report `0` (the leaf blocks a radius-20 body that would fit
without it), and the rest report `n` (too narrow even without a leaf). Monsters cannot open these doors, so a pack
behind a door cannot follow the player through it.

The goal's two conditions, "the player and monsters are blocked by open door leaves at about their visible size" and
"doorways stay passable", therefore conflict for stock-size monsters. Resolving it is a game design choice, so the
branch keeps the owner's rule as written: every body is blocked at the leaf's visible size.

Revision history: a second commit (0be8b388) let monsters wider than radius 14 walk through open leaves, through a
`CanCollideWith` override, and scored a radius-20 doorway check. The verifier rejected it as an unapproved design call
that brings back the walk-through behaviour the owner rejected. It is removed: `door_objects.zs` is again byte-identical
to the first commit (43e56ab2), `monster=` is an unscored diagnostic, and `action_regress.py` does not check it.
After the removal, `action_regress.py` passes everything (fresh conversions, main `uzdoom` build).
