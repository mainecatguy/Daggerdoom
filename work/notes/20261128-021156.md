# Lighting like Daggerfall Unity: darker dungeons, flat outdoor daylight (lightparity 01, run 20261128-021156)

Owner decisions: Q&A items 27, 29, 30 (`gamedesign/decisions/20261127-qa-batch4-owner.md`). Baseline: the visual
parity run (`work/notes/20261127-052057.md`, issues A1-A3).

Demo: `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-loop\review\lightparity\play.bat` (the paired game, built from this branch).
`compare.bat` opens `compare.html`: all 12 dungeon pairs before and after, and the outdoor shots before and after.

## Plan (as run)

1. Find how each side lights a dungeon, then sweep Doom's settings against the Daggerfall Unity shots of the
   visual parity run (same 12 spots in Privateer's Hold), using the real paired game.
2. Outdoors: give sprites the ground's flat daylight, the way `hw_flats.cpp` already lights the ground.
3. Prove it: matched dungeon pairs; outdoor shots by day, at night and on a snowy night with monsters among the trees;
   dynamic lights and light-amp still work; stock E1M1 unchanged.

## Findings

- **Our dungeon walls are the DFMesh layer**, lit flat at `sector light / 176` (`hw_dfmesh.cpp` `Ambient`) plus the
  dungeon's RDB light records as Doom dynamic lights. The dungeon script set every sector to 112 (`dd_dungeon_light`),
  so walls showed 0.64 of full everywhere. Nothing darkened with distance except the lights' own falloff.
- **Daggerfall Unity's look comes from two things**: a dark dungeon ambient of 0.12 (`PlayerAmbientLight`), and the
  **player torch**, which its default settings switch on in every dungeon (`PlayerTorchFromItems=False`,
  `EnablePlayerTorch.cs`; also in the capture build's `settings.ini`). The torch is a white point light, intensity 1,
  range 6 m, 0.6 m above the body's centre (`Prefabs/Player/PlayerAdvanced.prefab`). The torch is what makes near
  walls bright and far walls dark. The capture player moves the player to each camera spot, so its shots include it.
- **Scale check**: 1 Daggerfall Unity metre is 30 Doom units here (`MeshReader.GlobalScale` 0.025 x world scale
  0.75). `dd_lights.cpp` converts Unity light ranges as 75 x `dd_light_range` (0.4), which is the same 30, so the torch
  uses that conversion: reach 180 units, 45 units up.
- **Doors** (and other action models) are converter models with a `Bright` state (`door_objects.zs`,
  `action_objects.zs`), so they were fullbright: that is issue A3 ("doors show their planks; dark slabs in Daggerfall
  Unity").
- **Outdoors**, the ground already took Daggerfall's daylight flat (`DD_ExteriorGroundLight`), but sprites used Doom's
  sector light with distance darkening, so on a snowy night trees and monsters were black against grey snow.
- **An archived cvar's old value wins**: `FGameConfigFile::ReadCVars` applies ini lines whatever the flags, and the
  dungeon script reads `dd_dungeon_light` from converted data. Changing that default would do nothing for existing
  configs, and renaming it in the script would need every dungeon's data regenerated. So the engine now sets the
  ambient itself, from a new cvar.

## What changed (UZDoom only; active only on generated Daggerfall maps)

1. `playsim/dd_lights.cpp` `TickDungeon`: in a Daggerfall dungeon (`DDTreadmill_Place() == 3`) whose light records
   exist (`DFDungeonLight` actors), once per map or load: every sector gets `dd_dungeon_ambient` (32), and the player
   gets Daggerfall Unity's torch (`ddtorch`, white, radius 90/reach 180, 45 up, steady). Leaving the dungeon removes
   it. New cvars: `dd_dungeon_ambient` 32, `dd_torch` on, `dd_torch_range` 1. Log line
   `DDLIGHTS dungeon ambient 32, torch on` (debug print). The scan for light actors runs once per map, not per tic.
2. `hw_sprites.cpp` + `hw_flats.cpp`: outdoors, non-fullbright actor sprites (trees, flats, monsters) take the same
   flat daylight base light as the ground (`DD_FlatBaseLight`). Only the base light changes: dynamic lights still add
   on top, and fullbright frames, fullbright views (light-amp) and the fuzz style are untouched. Particles are not
   changed.
3. Same files: `DFDoorVisual`/`DFActionVisual` models are no longer fullbright. They take the DFMesh layer's light
   (daylight outdoors, `sector light / 176` inside) plus dynamic lights, so a door is as dark as its wall. This also
   applies in towns and building interiors, where those models were fullbright too.
4. Tools: `tools/lightparity/sweep.py` (dungeon settings sweep over `tools/visual_parity/scenes.py`, which gained
   `--uz-arg`) and `tools/lightparity/outdoor.py` (outdoor shots with frozen monsters at known distances).
5. `manual.md`: "Light and darkness".

- Mean absolute difference per spot: about 21 before, about 6 now (about 4 without the two outliers below). "64, no torch" matches the mean too but not the
  shape: Daggerfall Unity is bright near the player and dark far away, which only the torch gives (`s1_00`).
- Doors are now dark like Daggerfall Unity's (`final3\torch1\s1_10.side.png`, `s1_06`).
- Outliers: `s1_05` is a wall right beside a flickering light (it moved 84-98 between identical runs). `s1_06`: the
  harness could not teleport the player there (as in the original run), so the torch is somewhere else and the shot
  is darker. `s1_07` (a big cave room) stays darker than Daggerfall Unity at the far walls: Unity's light falloff
  differs from Doom's linear one. Not tuned further.
- Folders: `review\lightparity\sweep`, `sweep2` (settings sweep), `final3\torch1` (new defaults, door fix).
  The literal 0.12 ambient (21) is darker overall than Daggerfall Unity's shots; 32 fits best, so it is the default.

## Failed approaches and mistakes

- First sweep used the visual parity play folder: its engine was older than this branch's mod (`ModifyPellets`
  script error). Assembled `review\lightparity\play` from `review\batch12-stage` (this branch's head) with the new
  engine.
- Raising light range alone (0.4 -> 0.6) barely changed most spots: no dungeon light reaches them. That led to the torch.
- One final run still had ambient 21: I changed the default after copying the engine. Rebuilt and reran.
- Outdoor runs: some screenshots repeated the previous view, or the run timed out at startup; longer waits and a
  90 s timeout fixed it.

## Known gaps

- Daggerfall Unity also turns the torch on **outdoors at night** when city lights are on (`EnablePlayerTorch`). Not
  done; asked as a question.
- Dungeon billboards (furniture flats, candles) and monsters still use Doom's sector light with distance darkening at
  level 32, so far from any light they are darker than the walls. Monsters near the player are lit by the torch.
- Building interiors (`dd_interior_light` 48) were not compared with Daggerfall Unity (outside this goal).
- The HUD weapon is lit by the sector light and the torch as before.
- Rain and summer daylight values were not rechecked against Daggerfall Unity (the ground fit is from earlier runs).
