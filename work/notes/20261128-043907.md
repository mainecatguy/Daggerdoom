# QA bot can fly (qafly 01), 2026-11-28

Goal: the QA bot gets its own fly ability, behind its test flag, used only where only levitation would reach, and the
run summary lists where it flew. **Result: done.** In Mantellan Crux the bot reaches two levitation-only ledges by
flying, and `qa.py summary` lists every flight.

## What was built

- `UZDoom/src/dd_qa.cpp` (the QA bot's own file, inactive without `-ddqa`; no hook or game class changed):
  - New job `fly_to x y z [tolerance] [seconds]`. It turns on the player's own Doom flight (`MF2_FLY` and
    `MF_NOGRAVITY`, the flags the `fly` cheat sets) for this one job. It rises with the fly input (the fly-up key's
    channel), holding the take-off spot with move and strafe input while rising. Once above the goal floor it crosses
    at walking speed, turns flight off over the goal and lands. Results: `arrived` (landed on a floor at the goal
    height), `missed` (landed lower), `stuck`, `timeout`, `died`. Flight always ends with the job, including `stop`
    and replacement by another job.
  - Every flight reply says `"flew":true` with `fly_from`, and the outbox gets a `flew` event (from, to, result).
  - `move_to` with tolerance 8 or less (a precise stop) brakes like a player: no forward input, or a tap back, when the
    slide would overshoot. Doom keeps about 85 units of slide after walking, so without this the bot could never stand
    still on a take-off node. Ordinary `goto` waypoints (tolerance 12 to 24) are unchanged.
- `tools/qa/nav.py`: levitation links cost 1e6, so a plan uses as few flights as possible and only when needed. The
  plan is split into walk segments and fly segments (`segments`).
- `tools/qa/qa.py`:
  - `goto` plans walking first. Only if there is **no walking route** does it add levitation links and plan again
    (`needs_levitation: true`). It flies each levitation link with `fly_to` and lists every flight in the reply.
  - Before a flight the bot waits for its slide to stop and walks back onto the take-off node. It **refuses to fly**
    (`wrong_floor`) if it is not on the take-off node's floor, so flight can never start from an unplanned place.
  - If a flight stops below the ledge (the body is 32 wide on a 24-unit lattice, so rising beside a ledge can bump its
    underside), it takes off again 16, then 32 units further back, on the line from the ledge through the take-off node.
  - `qa.py summary` prints and writes `qa/summary.txt` with the setup commands used and every flight (including
    failed attempts).
  - `qa.py start --solo <files>` runs Doom alone on MAP01 of a converted dungeon (Doom runs without Daggerfall).
    `DDQA_VISIBLE=1` opens the window on the user's desktop; it is for the review demo only.
  - `start` empties the `qa` folder instead of removing it (a shell sitting in it made `rmtree` fail).

## Failed approaches and findings, in order

1. The first `fly_to` treated being within the tolerance horizontally as arrival, even below the ledge. Landing now
   waits until the bot is above the goal floor.
2. The first test spawned the player at z -384: Doom puts the player on the floor *under* the Crux entrance, and the
   walk graph's entrance node is on floor 936. The check teleports to the graph's entrance as a listed setup step.
   (Outside this goal: worth knowing for any Crux run. The `1001_player.pk3` in that conversion did not change it.)
3. Walking momentum carried the bot about 40 to 90 units past its last waypoint, sometimes under the ledge's slab, so
   flights started from the wrong place and bumped the slab. The fix has three parts: settle (wait until the position
   is the same in two different tics; my first version compared two replies from the same tic), precise braking, and
   walking back to the take-off node.
4. The first back-off retry backed away from the ledge's centre, which drove the bot further under the slab. It now
   backs off along the ledge-to-take-off line, because the take-off node is clear overhead in the graph.
5. **Walking gap (not flight):** `move_to` steers in 2D only. Where floors are stacked, the walk can end on another
   layer than the plan's node. Twice the walk toward a take-off node ended on the wrong floor (once on top of something
   at z 808 over floor 360, once on floor 936 where the graph drops to -384). The `wrong_floor` guard stopped both
   flights, as intended. Planning ledge 2 straight from ledge 1 hits this, so the check starts ledge 2 from the
   entrance. A layer-aware walk (checking z at each waypoint and re-planning) is QA bot walking work, not part of this
   goal.
6. The levitation links in `nav.py` check only the take-off node's ceiling, not the 8 neighbours its body overlaps.
   The back-off retry covers this in practice; a stricter link test could replace it later.
