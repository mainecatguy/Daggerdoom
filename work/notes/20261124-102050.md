## What was built

### Inputs (section 9.2 placements)

`DoomerfallEncounterRequest.placements` is a list of `DoomerfallEncounterPlacement`:

- the placement hash as two 32-bit halves (`idHi`, `idLo`), because ZScript has no 64-bit integers;
- the origin in Doom coordinates, the Daggerfall enemy type, and a quest flag;
- `walk`, the walking distance from the entrance (< 0 when unknown);
- `treasure`, the distance to the nearest treasure pile (< 0 when none);
- `fitRadius` and `fitHeight`, the largest monster that fits at the origin (0 when not measured).

Card 5 measures `walk`, `treasure` and the fit sizes. The test fakes them. A request with no placements gets genes
and armies only, as in card 2, so card 2's test is unchanged.

### Plan (all points are integers in hundredths)

Fields:

- `avgPoints`, `poolPoints`, `reservePoints`, `bossPoints`, `titanPoints`, `splitPoints`, `spentPoints`,
  `leftoverPoints` and `movedDropped`.
- `normalPack`, plus the swing gene's lone, giant and jitter rates.
- `bossStyle` and `bosses` (placement indices, deepest first). These are what the loot work needs.
- `titan`, `titanPlacement` and `titanWhy`.
- `squads` and `placements`.

Each planned placement has:

- its role: normal, lone, giant, boss, miniboss, titan or surprise;
- which surprise, if any;
- its side: A, B, or 2 for a local war;
- whether it starts awake, and an ambush flag;
- its squads, reserve, budget and spend;
- its mooks. Each mook has its monster, a member number from 0 to n-1, its 64-bit ID as two halves, and its cost.

### Order (6.2)

1. **Pool** = the area level's average (from the data file's level curve) × the number of placements.
2. **Reserve.** Each placement sets aside the cost of its cheapest army monster that fits, else the cheapest pool
   monster that fits, else the cheapest pool monster. If the reserve alone exceeds the pool, which happens with
   expensive armies at low levels, the dearest reserves fall back to the pool's cheapest monster.
3. **Bosses (6.4).**
   - **Count:** about one per 15 placements, ±1, clamped to 1 to 4.
   - **Style:** a roll picks one strong boss, several minibosses, or a strong deepest boss plus minibosses. Strong
     takes 4 to 8 times the average and a miniboss 2 to 4 times, in tenths.
   - **Where:** the deepest boss is the far placement (walk at least 75% of the farthest) closest to treasure, else
     the farthest. Other bosses are scored by walk, nearness to treasure, and distance from bosses already chosen.
     Without walking distances, bosses go at random spread-out placements (farthest-point sampling), with a warning.
   - **Cap:** bosses take at most half of the points left after the reserve, and are scaled down in small dungeons.
   - **Leader:** the strongest pool monster of an army kind that fits, at 3/4 of the boss budget or less, at the
     tier above the army's lean (`boss_tier_up`), then higher, then lower. The colour lock does not bind it. The
     escort is a squad from the army.
4. **Titan (6.3).**
   - A place-seeded roll against the data file's titan chance: 0 below 20, 10% at 20, 20% at 25, 30% at 30.
   - Only the `EX` group counts as titans. In a Civil War, only Civil War safe ones (6 of the 10 EX titans are).
   - At most one. Its cost must be at most 40% of the pool and within the points left after reserve and bosses. A
     dungeon that cannot pay gets none.
   - It stands alone at a random non-boss placement that it fits at.
   - In a Dragon's Den at level 25 or higher, the titan is always the deepest boss's leader, and the boss share still
     buys an escort. If the reserve leaves no room, the dearest reserves fall back to the pool's cheapest monster.
5. **Split (6.2 step 4).** Every remaining placement rolls a role:
   - **Lone:** only its reserve. The chance is 2% + 2.5% per swing setting.
   - **Giant:** 5 to 10 times the normal. The chance is 1% + 0.5% per swing setting.
   - **Normal:** its weight varies around 1 by ±(10% + 4% per swing setting).
   - **Surprises:** exceptions × 1% per placement. An empty wing (a seed placement and its nearest tenth keep only
     their one monster) happens in a dungeon with chance exceptions × 4%.
   - The split points go out in proportion to weight. The "normal pack" differs per dungeon because the share of
     lone placements does. If no placement has weight, for example a dungeon of one boss, the bosses take the split.
6. **Fill.** Bosses and the titan go first, then everything else, each placement in turn:
   - A placement reuses or invents a squad (Pitman-Yor, below). It buys members until none is affordable.
   - The size gene tilts picks toward cheap members (swarm) or dear ones (titan): the weight is multiplied by
     (rank+1)^tilt or (k-rank)^-tilt, with tilt from -3 to 3. So a heavy encounter is a bigger swarm or a bigger
     monster.
   - The first monster must fit at the origin (the leader, 9.3). If none can, the reserve monster stands there.
   - Unspent points carry to the next placement. The last carry buys the pool's cheapest monsters on the last
     placements. So spent matches the pool to within less than one cheapest monster. In every test plan the leftover
     was 0.2 to 1.25 points.
7. **Squads (section 7).** Pitman-Yor: reuse squad k with weight (uses_k − d), or invent one with weight
   (θ + d·K), with d = 0.5.
   - θ = `squad_strength` × the novelty gene's value from `novelty_theta`, which runs geometrically from 0.25 to 4.
   - A new squad is 1 to 3 army monsters picked by share, with weights 1 to 4.
   - Surprise squads do not count toward reuse.
8. **Surprise rooms (7.1).** Each is the opposite of the dungeon's pattern:
   - **wrongtier:** army kinds 3 or more tiers from the lean.
   - **wrongarmy:** a kind that neither army has.
   - **lonebrute:** in a swarm army, the dearest affordable tier 5+ monster, alone.
   - **swarm:** in a titan army, the pool's cheapest monster, many times over.
   - **sleepinghorde:** a giant-weight pack, all asleep.
   - **localwar:** united dungeons only. Half an army squad, half a wrongarmy squad, side 2. The placer must make the
     two halves hate each other.
   - **emptywing:** see the split above.
   - The "lone titan" surprise is left out: EX titans stay under the titan rule (6.3), and DRLA bosses are still
     unpriced (open question).
9. **Awareness (9.2).** Per placement, awake with the awareness word's chance: asleep 0, mostlyasleep 25%, mixed
   50%, mostlyalert 75%, alert 100%, sentries 40%. Ambush placements are asleep with the ambush flag.

### Hooks for the placer (6.6)

- `DoomerfallEncounterPlanner.Promote(plan, placement, maxCount)` keeps the first mook (the leader, chosen to fit) and
  rebuys the placement's points with at most `maxCount` army-kind monsters that fit there. Each pick is the cheapest
  one that reaches the average cost needed per remaining slot. It returns the points it could not buy, and **the
  placer must pass them to Move.** Members are renumbered from 0, so the same calls give the same IDs.
- `DoomerfallEncounterPlanner.Move(plan, from, points, full)` gives the points to the nearest placement not marked
  full, as more of that placement's squad, then the pool's cheapest monsters. With every placement full, it returns
  -1 and adds the points to `plan.movedDropped`, which a test should report.

### Mook IDs: member bits widened from 8 to 12

Today's layout was tag 3 | placement hash 53 | member 8. A level 30 swarm army gives packs of more than 256: in the
test, a level 30 boss had 248 mooks and a stock level 30 boss had 301. The new layout is **tag 3 | placement hash
49 | member 12**, which allows 4,096 per placement. The planner stops adding mooks at 4,096.

The changes:

- `bridge/doomerfall_bridge.h`: `DFB_MONSTER_MEMBER_BITS 12u`. `generate_bindings.py` was rerun, which regenerated
  `DoomerfallBridge.g.cs` and `doomerfall_bridge_check.g.h`.
- `DoomerfallMonsters.cs`: `PlacementMask` and a new `MemberMask` come from the header constants. This replaces the
  hard-coded `53` and two `& 0xff`.
- `tools/dungeon_converter/EnemyRoster.cs`: `MonsterId` uses 12 member bits and a 49-bit hash.
- `UZDoom/src/dd_monsters.cpp`: the member offset uses the header constant instead of `& 0xff`.
- ZScript: `DoomerfallEncounterPlanner.MookId` builds the same layout from two 32-bit halves. The Python test checks
  it against a 64-bit reference.

Consequences (no workaround needed during exploratory development):

- **Maps must be reconverted.** Converted maps hold the old IDs in their DDMONST lump.
- **Old death ledgers no longer match.** `DoomerfallMonsters.json` in old saves keys deaths by the old IDs.
- **Old builds stop working together.** The bridge layout hash includes the header constants, so old launchers and
  bridge DLLs no longer pair with new builds.
- **Collisions:** 49 hash bits still make a collision between two placements in one dungeon vanishingly unlikely.

## What felt off (for the owner)

- **Cheap armies make huge boss escorts.** A strong boss takes 4 to 8 times the average, and the leader takes at most
  3/4 of it. In a level 30 army of 1.75-point monsters, the escort is about 200 mooks. This is the design followed
  literally. See the question.
- **Low levels are almost all lone monsters.** At level 1 (3.5 points per encounter), each placement's reserve is
  usually most of its average (a Crypt reserved 66.6 of 70). Most placements get one monster and a few get two to
  four. That follows from the level curve.
- **The size gene's swarm and titan tilt is strong.** A titan-leaning army buys its dearest affordable member first,
  so mid-sized placements often hold one big monster. Packs in the Dragon's Dens had a median of 2.
- **The regional favourite still costs points** (card 2's open question). Nothing here special-cases it. At low
  levels it often cannot be afforded beyond a reserve.

## Engineering calls made

- Bosses take at most half of the points left after the reserve.
- The deepest boss is chosen among placements with walk at least 75% of the farthest.
- Weights for choosing other bosses: walk 1,000, treasure nearness 500, spacing 1,000 (per 2,048 map units).
- The titan comes after the bosses in the order, but its 40% cap is on the whole pool.
- A Dragon's Den titan may push the dearest reserves down to the pool's cheapest monster.
- Only the `EX` group counts as titans for 6.3. In Civil Wars, only Civil War safe ones.
- Swing gene rates, surprise and empty-wing rates, and the novelty θ table are in the data file.
- The boss leader is exempt from the colour lock, because it is a tier up by design.
- The planner caps members at 4,096 per placement. Points over that carry to the next placement.

## Not done here

- Dead mooks (`deadMookIds`) are not read. The plan is the same with or without deaths, and the placer skips dead IDs
  (9.2).
- Nothing reads the plan yet. The placer is card 5.

## Failed approaches

- The test handler was missing from MAPINFO's `AddEventHandlers` at first, so the first two runs printed nothing.
- In the first fill, each mook rebuilt its weight array. It was fine once the handler ran (0.2 s for 510 plans), but
  costs and weights are now computed once per squad buy.
- A single-placement dungeon lost its split, because no placement had split weight. The bosses now take it.
- A level 27 Civil War Dragon's Den with a titan-size army reserved 85% of its pool, so no titan fit within 40%. This
  led to the reserve fallback for Dens.
