# Persistent townspeople

## Plan and design

Daggerfall's wanderers become a fixed roster per town, generated from the town's map id and the world time. Daggerfall
generates and owns them; Doom only draws the ones near the player.

- **Where it lives.** Generation is pure C# in `daggerfall-unity-master/Assets/Scripts/Doomerfall/Townsfolk.cs`. The
  bridge glue and death ledger are in `DoomerfallTownspeople.cs`. Doom's side is `UZDoom/src/dd_townsfolk.cpp`. Other
  agents need only the bridge kinds 64 to 68 and `DoomerfallTownspeople.FindPerson(id)`.
- **Why Daggerfall generates, not Doom.** Daggerfall owns permanent state, and the dialogue task will talk to these
  people from Daggerfall. Generating in one place means the schedule exists once, with no second copy to keep equal.
- **Seed and id.** Town seed = hash of the map id. Person `i` gets `id = tag 7 | hash(map id, i)`. Nothing else is
  needed to regenerate a person, so the save holds only the dead ids (`DoomerfallTownspeople.json` beside the other
  ledgers).
- **Lazy.** A town's roster is built the first time the player is in it outdoors (a few ms). Each person's daily plan is
  built only when asked about, and a walking path only for someone whose walk passes within view of the player.
- **Population.** Daggerfall's own rule: 24 wanderers per 16 blocks (up to 96) within 150 m of the player. That density
  is spread over the whole town and doubled, since about half the town is indoors at a daytime moment. Gothway-sized
  towns get tens of people; Daggerfall city about 1,200 (the cap).
- **Appearance.** Daggerfall's `MobilePersonNPC.RandomiseNPC` rule: 1 in 32 is a guard, otherwise gender 50/50, outfit
  1 of 4, race from the climate. The billboard archives are the ones `MobilePersonBillboard` uses. Names come from
  Daggerfall's name generator, seeded per person, and are only made when asked for.
- **Places.** Every real building in the town, each with a walkable navigation cell beside it. Homes are houses
  (House1 to House6). Each person also has their own spot near each place, so a crowd does not stack on one cell.
- **Habits** (the variety, kept to six simple types):
  - Worker (36%): home, then a workplace near home until about 17:30, often a tavern after.
  - Homebody (22%): mostly near home, an occasional trip to one nearby place.
  - Roamer (28%): drifts between three places in their part of town.
  - Night owl (10%): wakes mid-afternoon and keeps the tavern busy past midnight.
  - Pacer (4%): crosses town between two far places at almost all hours. The rare surprising one.
  - Guard (1 in 32): patrols four stops for a 12-hour shift, a third of them at night.
- **Schedules.** Waking and sleeping times are per person (about 6:30 and 21:30, with spread). Asleep means inside the
  home, so not shown. Each day's plan is rebuilt from (person, day): the anchors stay, the order and timing vary.
  While at a place, each stretch of time is inside (hidden) or outside by the person's own outdoors habit. Outside, the
  person stands or paces between two nearby spots with pauses, as a periodic function of time.
- **Walking.** A* over Daggerfall's city navigation grid (the same rules as `CityNavigation.SetRMBData`: buildings and
  water are blocked, roads preferred), then straightened. The plan fixes the walk's start and end time, so position is
  still a pure function of time.
- **Doom.** Daggerfall sends the nearest 128 visible people within 150 m every half second, as position and velocity
  (kinds 64 to 66). Doom keeps one passive `DFTownsperson` per person, extrapolates between lists, puts it on the
  floor, and picks Daggerfall's walking sprite by viewing angle (idle record when standing). The id is in
  `ddRecordId`, so a death reaches Daggerfall through the existing monster death hook (kind 67).
- **Converter.** The runtime package now holds the mobile billboard archives 381-399 and 451-456 with all frames, their
  scales in a `DFMOBILE` lump, and the `DFTownsperson` class.

## Verification

Run with `python tools/townspeople/townspeople.py run NAME [--hour H] [--kill --save | --load]`. Logs are in
`review/townspeople/run/`.

- **Same town, same people, in separate launches.** The census digest covers every person's id, archive, home, habit,
  and waking hours. It was `b771bd17eaae2dc5` in all three launches (day1, kill, load). At 12:00 the first rows matched
  too: for example `ea6a534bf1e3df71` stood at 6108.2,2092.0 in both day1 and kill. The visible-list digests differ by a
  few game seconds because the clock is set when Daggerfall reaches play, not at a fixed frame.
- **People return to their usual places.** The first 40 people were sampled at 9:00, 12:00, 15:00, and 18:00 over 7
  days. In 906 of 1033 awake samples they were at their home or one of their own haunts; the rest were walking between
  them. Per person, for example: worker 9 was at own places 28/28, a roamer 26/28, and the pacer only 4/28 (intended).
- **Movement.** Doom showed 16 townspeople. Over 3 seconds, walkers moved (for example `fa82cc0b32daa2e3` went from
  1850,-13312 to 1836,-13281) while standers stayed put. Sprites switch between walk records (r00 to r04) and idle (r05).
- **A death stays dead after save and load.** The kill run used a test-only `Die()` call from the harness in `tools/`.
  Doom logged `DDTOWNSFOLK died ea6a534bf1e3df71`, and Daggerfall logged `town: death ea6a534bf1e3df71 Lysyssa Ashhouse
  (Roamer)`. Doom's count fell from 15 to 14. The harness then autosaved. The load run logged `town: ledger restored with
  1 deaths` and then `dead 1` in every report, and the id never appeared in Doom's log after the load.
- **Stock DOOM.WAD E1M1**: no errors and no bridge lines. **DRLA E1M1**: the same 3 script errors and 763 unknown-class
  lines as the previous build (`townspeople.py check`). Plain Daggerfall launch: no bridge mentions and no exceptions.

## Failed approaches and problems found

- A harness that used the literal `"@MODE@".IndexOf(...)` made UZDoom's ZScript compiler hang at "LoadActors" with no
  error. A `String` variable fixed it. Worth knowing for other test harnesses.
- `quiet_run.py` returns 57 for a normal `-norun` start without `-stdout`, so its exit code is not a startup check.

## Design calls to review

- The density uses Daggerfall's rule times 1.9, so about as many people are in view as Daggerfall's own pool. This is
  an ordinary engineering estimate, easy to change in `TownRoster` (`perBlock`).
- Habit mix and schedule numbers are my judgment within the owner's description. They are constants near the top of
  `MakePerson`.
- Indoor townspeople are not drawn inside buildings. See the question in the report.

## Play-test list

1. Walk around Gothway by day. Do the people look like Daggerfall's wanderers, at the right size, on the ground?
2. Does the walking sprite face the way the person walks? If people seem to walk sideways or moonwalk, the mirror
   choice in `ChooseSprite` (dd_townsfolk.cpp) is backwards.
3. Do walking people stay on roads and out of buildings?
4. Rest until night. Are the streets mostly empty, with a few night owls and guards?
5. Walk out of Gothway into the countryside and back in. Are the same kinds of people about, with no one popping in
   right beside you?
6. Stand still a while. Does anyone pop in or out of view nearby? (They should only appear past about 150 m, or at a
   door when going in or out.)
