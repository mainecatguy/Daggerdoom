# Climbing sticks to steep stair-like surfaces (20261126-223019)

Goal: in Privateer's Hold the player climbs the steep "cliff" bottom to top in one continuous climb; ordinary walls
and walkable slopes behave as before. Branch `agent/climb-20261126-223019`. Mod-only change (ZScript), no engine change.

## Plan (as run)

1. Find the cliff in the converted map and reproduce the drop-out without Daggerfall.
2. Keep the climb going across a narrow ledge when another climbable surface is right behind it (owner's suggestion).
3. Add scripted tests for the steep stairs and for a wide ledge (old behaviour kept), rerun the old climb tests and
   stock E1M1, and leave a demo.

## Findings

- **Where the cliff is.** Privateer's Hold's collision is a 12-unit grid with 3D floors, so I could not read it from
  the TEXTMAP alone. `tools/climb/find_steep_stairs.py` floods the walkable area from the cave a new character wakes in
  (510,1672, floor 960) and looks for stacks of risers taller than Doom's 24-unit step with short treads. Only one
  turned up, the same with risers capped at 72 or 160: a staircase at x 800-942, y -144..-48. It has 11 risers of 32 units
  with 12-unit treads (about 69 degrees), from floor 384 to 768. I took this to be the owner's "cliff". It matches
  "on inspecting the meshes these are very steep stairs". The map analysis found three more copies of the same
  staircase in other rotations elsewhere in the dungeon (x -768..-624 y 1392..1488, x -1488..-1392 y 576..720,
  x 48..144 y -960..-816). The fix applies to them too.
- **Why it let go.** The climb traces for the wall 20 units above the feet. After about 46 units of climbing, that
  trace passes over the riser's top and the next riser is 12 units farther, beyond the touch distance. The surface
  "ended", so the climb did its top-out hop onto the ledge and stopped. On the 12-unit tread the player had to push for
  another second (and win another start roll) to climb again. Reproduced with
  `tools/climb/run_cliff_probe.py` on the old code: **4 separate climbs** to get up the cliff (walking), each about 46
  units of climbing then a hop.
- **Running was worse.** At run climb speed (5.56/tic) the climb kept hold of a riser 12 units away while the feet rose
  past the tread between. The top-out found no ledge in reach and simply let go (also 4 climbs).

## What was built

`mods/doomerfall/src/zscript/DoomerfallClimb.zs`:

- The surface can end while climbing with no strafe input and not slipping, with forward held or no input at all.
  Then `CrossNarrowLedge` traces again at the same height, with enough reach to find a surface behind a tread. If it
  finds another climbable surface (wall, 3D-floor side, or a plane of 70+ degrees), it measures the **tread**: the
  floor just in front of that surface, measured back to where the floor leaves that height. The climb continues only
  if the tread is **at most `NarrowLedge` = 16 units deep**, sits no more than a step above the feet, and is not a
  gap (more than 48 below the feet). Forward rises to the tread at climb speed, then crosses toward the new surface
  at climb speed, and the normal climb takes over at the next riser. No input stays put (no roll, no slip, as before).
- **Strafing never enters the crossing.** Strafing off a surface's end or corner tops out or lets go exactly as before.
  Strafing from the middle of a crossing does the same: the player is not held in place.
- Version 1 (rejected by the verifier) used a 32-unit limit measured from the player's center. That accepted ledges up
  to about 44 units deep, so walls with a standable ledge or parapet kept climbing. It also froze a strafing player.
  Both are fixed above.
- Rising before crossing means Doom's automatic step-up adds no free height. My first version crossed right away
  and climbed the cliff about 1.5x faster than the climb speed.
- Tics spent crossing a ledge with forward held count toward Daggerfall's continue roll every 30 tics, as climbing does.
  Without that, a steep-stair climb would roll less often than a wall climb. The tests and probes were rerun after this
  change: 17/17, and one climb walking and running.
- Everything else is unchanged. A ledge deeper than 16 units, nothing climbable behind it, or strafe input gets the
  old top-out hop or let-go. Back (climbing down), slipping, jumping off, and the skill rolls are untouched.

## Verification

- **Cliff probe** (`python tools/climb/run_cliff_probe.py <uzdoom.exe> <data> <out> [--run] [--chances ...]`, the
  converted Privateer's Hold map with no Daggerfall; the probe sends the rule and chance events a bridge session sends):
  - Old code, walking, 100% chances: `starts=4 stops=4`, topped at tic 241.
  - New code, walking: `starts=1 stops=1` (the stop is the final top-out at 752 onto 768), topped at tic 204. That is
    334 units of rise in 141 tics, about 2.4/tic against a climb speed of 2.78.
  - New code, running: `starts=1 stops=1`, topped at tic 133.
  - New code, a new character's real chances (76/48/57/29): one continue roll failed at z 592. The player slipped onto
    the tread below, which ends the climb (Daggerfall's rule: a slip that reaches the ground ends it). After pushing again,
    the second climb went to the top in one go. So with real skill a slip can still interrupt; see the question below.
- **Scripted climb tests** (`python quiet_run.py --timeout 200 -- python tools/climb/run_test.py <uzdoom.exe> <work>`):
  **19/19 PASS** after the verifier fixes. That covers the 14 old checks (wall start after 1 s, speed, top-out, hold,
  fire, jump, strafe, uses, slip, 75-degree slope climbed, 60-degree slope walked, rules off) plus 5 new ones on the
  hand-made map:
  - a 32/12 staircase is one climb, walking;
  - the same, running;
  - a 64-unit ledge still ends the climb (rested 41 tics, then a new climb);
  - a 24-unit ledge, above the 16-unit limit and under the player's width, still ends the climb (rested 32 tics);
  - strafing from mid-crossing on the staircase is not held (dy -17.6; the climb tops out onto the tread as before).
  I did not run the two newest checks against the version-1 code to prove they would have failed it.
- After the fixes, the cliff probe is still one climb walking (topped at tic 204) and running (topped at tic 133), and
  E1M1 is unchanged: 0 errors stock, and the same 16 DRLA errors with and without the mod.
- **Stock E1M1** (DOOM.WAD, 25 s each, private config, quiet_run): stock and stock+mod give 0 error lines. DRLA set and
  DRLA+mod give the same 16 error lines (DRLA's own `Midas_Statue_Fatality`/translation errors). No climb lines anywhere.
  No engine change was made.
- Not run: a paired session with Daggerfall. The change is pure ZScript inside the climb, and the bridge parts
  (`dfi_climb`, `dfc_uses`) are untouched.

## Failed approaches

- Scanning ahead for "the first ledge no lower than the feet" in the top-out. It fixed nothing when running, because
  the feet were already above the tread. It was replaced by the extended trace plus "highest floor between".
- Parsing the TEXTMAP for steep stairs found only flat-sector staircases and stray 8x8 slope cells, because the dungeon's
  real collision heights live in 3D floors. The in-game flood (`find_steep_stairs.zs`) was the reliable way.

## Judgment calls

- `NarrowLedge` = 16, measured as tread depth. That is just over the cliff's 12-unit treads (measured in 2-unit
  samples) and half the player's width. Any ledge a player could stand on tops out as before. The owner's words:
  "a way too steep staircase".
- Climbing down a too-steep staircase still ends at each tread, because reaching a floor while climbing down ends the
  climb. Climbing down is an open owner question already, so I left it.

## Play-test list

Demo: `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-loop\review\climb-steep-stairs\play.bat` (Doom only, starts at the foot of the cliff
facing it, with a new character's real climb chances).

- Hold forward: one continuous climb to the top? Does crossing each narrow tread (rise, then a short forward
  move) look and feel right, or does it look like stair-stepping?
- Try it running. The climb is faster, as before.
- Let go mid-cliff: the player should hang still. Press forward again to carry on.
- A failed roll (likely with 57% continue) slips onto a tread and ends the climb; you have to push again.
  `netevent dfi_climb:100:100:100:100:0` in the console removes slips for comparison.
- Ordinary walls (any town wall in a paired game) should still top out with the old hop.
