## What was built

- `dd_treadmill.cpp/.h`:
  - `DDTreadmill_HorizonFlats(px, north, out)`: one map pixel's flats as render data (tile corner, sink, texture,
    sprite scale), from the same `DDNature_Layout` call, archive (`DDNature_Archive`, season included) and scale table
    as `SpawnNature`.
  - `DDTreadmill_HorizonMapHasFlat(gx, gy)`: the same test `SpawnNature` uses for what the map spawns itself (the
    overworld window's tiles, or a town map's `Near(margin)` area).
  - `NatureOf`'s body moved into `LayoutNature`, so the ring can lay pixels out without filling (and clearing) the
    treadmill's own 16-pixel cache. `NatureOf` behaves as before.
- `hw_ddhorizon.cpp`:
  - Per-pixel cache of flats with sprite size, offsets and the coarse ground height (a pure function of the world tile,
    since the coarse grid is aligned to the world). A cache only; pixels the ring has left are dropped.
  - Each flat is a quad sized and offset like its sprite (`DFNatureFlat` actors use the same texture and scale), turned
    to face the middle of the camera's 32-tile chunk, alpha tested at 0.5 like sprites, lit like the ring terrain.
    One draw call per texture (31 per climate archive).
  - A flat on the map's own tiles (a town map's ground beyond its 24-tile nature margin) or in the fine band stands on
    the exact ground; elsewhere on the coarse quad's triangle, so it never floats or sinks against the drawn ring.
  - Scheduling: at most one heavy step per frame. The terrain when the key changes; else one missing pixel's layout,
    nearest first; else the flats buffer. Until the flats are built again the previous ones stay drawn in their own
    world place (their own anchor), so a chunk crossing shows no gap.
- No new CVAR. `dd_horizon 0` turns off terrain and flats; `dd_natureflats 0` leaves the ring's flats off for pixels
  laid out after it changes (same rule as the actors).

### Owner rules

- Nothing is saved or stored about terrain objects: the ring's flats come from the same pure function of the map
  pixel as the actors. No serialization was added.
- Same objects at the same positions on every map: both the actors and the ring call `DDNature_Layout` with the same
  inputs (`LayoutNature` is `NatureOf`'s body). The ring draws exactly the flats the map does not spawn.
- Places Daggerfall keeps clear stay clear: the ring uses the layout's own exclusions (location rectangles with
  clearance, and the tile types). Roads: vanilla Daggerfall Unity has no wilderness road layer (that is the Basic
  Roads mod), and `terrainflats` follows vanilla, so there is nothing more to respect here. If a road layer is added
  later, it belongs in `DDNature_Layout`, and the ring follows it for free.

### Cost

From the `DDHORIZON` log lines. Other lanes were loading this machine during these runs.

- Flats in the ring: about 77,000 in woodland (overworld), 59,000 at Privateer's Hold, and 72,000 to 74,000 around
  Daggerfall. That is two triangles each, drawn with one draw call per texture (31).
- Laying out one map pixel: 3 to 21 ms, at most one per frame. About 26 to 30 pixels on a new map or after a warp,
  so the distant flats fill in over about half a second. A chunk crossing needs 0 to 2 new pixels.
- Ring terrain rebuild: 6 to 15 ms (first build on a map: 13 to 324 ms, cold height cache, during the load).
- Flats buffer rebuild: 10 to 23 ms, once at 59.5 ms and once at 69 ms (both on a frame right after a map start or a
  shift). This runs on its own frame, one frame after the terrain. **Performance risk:** this is a single-frame hitch
  of about 10 to 20 ms each time the camera crosses a 32-tile chunk line (about every 10 s at a run). It costs more
  than the terrain rebuild. Ways to cut it, not done: an index buffer (4 vertices per quad instead of 6), or
  per-pixel vertex ranges so that only pixels new to the ring are rebuilt (that needs camera-independent facing,
  such as crossed quads, which would change the look).
- Frame time: `tools/nature/frame_cost.py --toggle dd_horizon` (new `--toggle` option), four sessions on/off/on/off
  in woodland at 1280x720. Medians: view on 15.5 ms standing, 15.6 ms running. View off 17.1 ms standing, 20.0 ms
  running. Machine load makes the noise larger than any cost of the ring, so the draw cost is **not resolved**, only
  shown to be small on this machine. Logs: `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-build\distantlod-tests\framecost`.

### Known gaps

- Facing: a ring flat faces the middle of the camera's chunk, not the camera. Near the ring's inner edge (32 or more
  tiles away) a flat can be turned up to about 40 degrees from the camera, which makes it look narrower. I could not
  see this in the screenshots.
- After a treadmill shift, the old flats buffer is drawn for a frame or two until the new one is built. Flats on
  tiles the window has just taken over are then drawn twice in the same spot (the ring quad and the actor). The
  window's own new chunks spawn their actors over a few tics (`ProcessPending`), and the ring excludes those tiles
  right away, so the far side of a freshly shifted window can be empty of flats for a few tics. Both happen 32 or
  more tiles from the player.
- Still one ground texture in the ring (no water, dirt or climate), as noted in the first run. Desert and mountain
  ring flats use their own climate archives, but the ground under them stays green.
- Distant towns (`distant-town-view` backlog item) are not drawn.
- The far edge is still the 60,000-unit circle.
