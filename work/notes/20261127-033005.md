# Distant terrain LOD, second run: distant objects (20261127-033005)

Follows `work/notes/20261126-213115.md` (the render-only terrain ring, `hw_ddhorizon.cpp`). That run left distant
objects and the Daggerfall Unity comparison open. `terrainflats` has since landed: wilderness nature flats laid out by
`DDNature_Layout` (`dd_nature.cpp`), a pure function of the map pixel, never saved.

## Plan

1. Draw, on the ring, every nature flat the running map does not spawn as an actor, from the same layout rule.
2. Keep frame hitches small: no single frame should do the ring terrain, a pixel layout and the flats together.
3. Screenshots: overworld woodland (from a hilltop height, view on and off, before and after a treadmill shift),
   Privateer's Hold exterior, a town map (the townlookback gap), and a Daggerfall Unity view of the same kind.
4. Stock checks (DOOM.WAD E1M1 plain and DRLA), frame cost, demo.

## What was built

- `dd_treadmill.cpp/.h`:
  - `DDTreadmill_HorizonFlats(px, north, out)`: one map pixel's flats as render data (tile corner, sink, texture,
    sprite scale), from the same `DDNature_Layout` call, archive (`DDNature_Archive`, season included) and scale table
    as `SpawnNature`.
  - `DDTreadmill_HorizonMapHasFlat(gx, gy)`: the same test `SpawnNature` uses for what the map spawns itself (the
    overworld window's tiles, or a town map's `Near(margin)` area).
  - `NatureOf`'s body moved into `LayoutNature`, so the ring can lay pixels out without filling (and clearing) the
    treadmill's own 16-pixel cache. `NatureOf` behaves as before.
- `hw_ddhorizon.cpp`:
  - Per-pixel cache of flats with sprite size, offsets and the coarse ground height (a pure function of the world tile,
    since the coarse grid is aligned to the world). A cache only; pixels the ring has left are dropped.
  - Each flat is a quad sized and offset like its sprite (`DFNatureFlat` actors use the same texture and scale),
    alpha tested at 0.5 like sprites, and lit like the ring terrain. One draw call per texture (31 per climate archive).
  - Facing (fixed after verifier review, round 2):
    - **Near flats** are those within 96 tiles of the middle of the camera's 32-tile chunk. They are kept in their own
      list and buffer, and their quads are turned to the camera's actual position again whenever the camera has moved
      one tile (192 units). This matters most on town and dungeon exterior maps: there the ring's flats start just
      outside the map's 24-tile nature margin, which is 4 to 12 tiles from a player standing between the exit and
      entry lines. At 4 tiles, one tile of movement turns a flat at most about 14 degrees from the camera, so a flat
      can never go edge-on.
    - **Far flats** (96 tiles or more out) face the chunk's middle. The camera is at most 23 tiles from it, so the
      error is at most about 14 degrees.
    - Round 1 faced every flat to the chunk's middle. On a town map a flat 16 tiles away could be exactly edge-on to
      the camera (the verifier's worked case).
  - A flat on the map's own tiles (a town map's ground beyond its 24-tile nature margin) or in the fine band stands on
    the exact ground; elsewhere on the coarse quad's triangle, so it never floats or sinks against the drawn ring.
  - Scheduling: at most one heavy step per frame. The terrain when the key changes; else one missing pixel's layout,
    nearest first; else the flats buffer. Until the flats are built again the previous ones stay drawn in their own
    world place (their own anchor), so a chunk crossing shows no gap.
- No new CVAR. `dd_horizon 0` turns off terrain and flats; `dd_natureflats 0` leaves the ring's flats off for pixels
  laid out after it changes (same rule as the actors).

### Owner rules

- Nothing is saved or stored about terrain objects: the ring's flats come from the same pure function of the map
  pixel as the actors. No serialization was added.
- Same objects at the same positions on every map: both the actors and the ring call `DDNature_Layout` with the same
  inputs (`LayoutNature` is `NatureOf`'s body). The ring draws exactly the flats the map does not spawn.
- Places Daggerfall keeps clear stay clear: the ring uses the layout's own exclusions (location rectangles with
  clearance, and the tile types). Roads: vanilla Daggerfall Unity has no wilderness road layer (that is the Basic
  Roads mod), and `terrainflats` follows vanilla, so there is nothing more to respect here. If a road layer is added
  later, it belongs in `DDNature_Layout`, and the ring follows it for free.

### Cost

These numbers come from the `DDHORIZON` log lines of rounds 1 and 2. Other lanes were loading this machine during
these runs. Round 3 (verifier: too much measurement code in game source) removed the per-pixel layout timing and the
flats build timing from `hw_ddhorizon.cpp`. The flats build now logs only one line: its tile, the near count and the
far texture count. The terrain build's timing line is from the first run and unchanged. The flats and layout figures
below cannot be reproduced with the current code without putting that instrumentation back temporarily.

- Flats in the ring: about 77,000 in woodland (overworld), 59,000 at Privateer's Hold, and 72,000 to 74,000 around
  Daggerfall. That is two triangles each, drawn with one draw call per texture (31).
- Laying out one map pixel: 3 to 21 ms, at most one per frame. About 26 to 30 pixels on a new map or after a warp,
  so the distant flats fill in over about half a second. A chunk crossing needs 0 to 2 new pixels.
- Ring terrain rebuild: 6 to 15 ms (first build on a map: 13 to 324 ms, cold height cache, during the load).
- Flats buffer rebuild: 10 to 23 ms, once at 59.5 ms and once at 69 ms (both on a frame right after a map start or a
  shift). This runs on its own frame, one frame after the terrain. **Performance risk:** this is a single-frame hitch
  of about 10 to 20 ms each time the camera crosses a 32-tile chunk line (about every 10 s at a run). It costs more
  than the terrain rebuild. Ways to cut it, not done: an index buffer (4 vertices per quad instead of 6), or
  per-pixel vertex ranges so that only pixels new to the ring are rebuilt (that needs camera-independent facing,
  such as crossed quads, which would change the look).
- Near flats (round 2): 3,000 to 3,700 on the overworld, 5,500 at Privateer's Hold, 6,300 on the town map. Their
  quads are rebuilt into a small buffer whenever the camera moves one tile, about 3 times a second at a run. I did
  not time that rebuild separately. It does the same per-flat work as the far build, for about a twelfth as many
  flats, so roughly 1 to 2 ms by proportion (unmeasured). The far build was 10.6 to 14.4 ms in round 2, and once
  83 to 104 ms in a run where everything, including the terrain at 48 to 70 ms, was slow from machine load.
- Frame time (measured with the round 1 build, before the near-flat change): `tools/nature/frame_cost.py --toggle dd_horizon` (new `--toggle` option), four sessions on/off/on/off
  in woodland at 1280x720. Medians: view on 15.5 ms standing, 15.6 ms running. View off 17.1 ms standing, 20.0 ms
  running. Machine load makes the noise larger than any cost of the ring, so the draw cost is **not resolved**, only
  shown to be small on this machine. Logs: `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-build\distantlod-tests\framecost`.

### Known gaps

- Facing: a flat turns away from the camera by at most about 14 degrees (see "Facing" above). The near quads turn
  in steps, once per tile of movement. That is a small rotation of trees 4 or more tiles away, and I have not judged
  how it looks in motion (play-test item 6).
- On the overworld (only), after a treadmill shift, the old flats buffer is drawn for a frame or two until the new one is built. Flats on
  tiles the window has just taken over are then drawn twice in the same spot (the ring quad and the actor). The
  window's own new chunks spawn their actors over a few tics (`ProcessPending`), and the ring excludes those tiles
  right away, so the far side of a freshly shifted window can be empty of flats for a few tics. On the overworld both
  happen 32 or more tiles from the player. On a town or dungeon exterior map, the map's own area does not move while
  the player is on it, so this does not occur there. Crossing a chunk line there briefly (for a frame or two) keeps
  the previous chunk's far buffer, which holds the same flats.
- Still one ground texture in the ring (no water, dirt or climate), as noted in the first run. Desert and mountain
  ring flats use their own climate archives, but the ground under them stays green.
- Distant towns (`distant-town-view` backlog item) are not drawn.
- The far edge is still the 60,000-unit circle.

## Play-test list

1. From a hilltop in woodland, look around. Do the distant trees read as forest continuing to the horizon? Is there
   a visible line where the real trees (actors) end and the ring's trees begin?
2. Run straight for a minute. Any visible hitch when crossing chunk lines, and do far trees jump or pop?
3. Walk into the City of Daggerfall from the west (`play-city.bat`) and turn around. Does the forest behind you look
   the same as it did from the overworld?
4. Privateer's Hold exterior: look past the map edge in each direction.
5. Desert and mountain areas: do the distant flats match the climate (sparse dead trees, pines)?
6. On a town map, walk along the edge of the town's ground (near where the overworld takes over) and look out. Do the
   nearest ring trees (just past the real ones) visibly turn as you walk (a small step once per tile)? Do they ever
   look narrower than the real trees?
