# Trapdoor shaft floor at the pillar's base (20261124-003059)

## Result

Under a lever trapdoor whose shaft has no Daggerfall geometry below, the converter now puts the shaft floor at the base of the hidden pillar on the tile's own chain, not 1,024 units down. The pillar still rises and the tile still flips, with the same numbers as before in the regression test.

Only the converter changed (`ActionExport.cs`, `Program.cs`). No engine or ZScript files changed, so nothing new runs without a generated map.

Nobody has played this by hand.

## The rule

- While reading the dungeon's geometry, the converter now records every action object by block and file position: its chain link, and, for a moving model, its box at rest (`NoteActionLink`).
- For each bottomless flat hinged tile (`FloorBottomlessPits`), it follows the tile's chain (`HiddenMoverBase`). It collects every action 1 to 7 model whose resting box overlaps the tile's footprint and whose top is at or below the tile's top. The shaft floor goes at the **lowest base** among them, so the frame (K0A) and every stacked pillar segment (KXD, KXE in Direnni Tower) start on the floor.
- If the chain hides nothing, the old 1,024-unit floor stays as the fallback.
- The converter logs each floored shaft, with its old and new floor height.

Chains are followed only inside a block, as in `RDBLayout`. A chain loop stops at the first repeat.

## Census (`DungeonConverter --list-trapdoors`, new `SHAFT` lines)

For each hinged kind, the census now counts the flat tiles whose chain hides an action 1 to 7 model under them. It does not know which tiles are bottomless, because that needs the full geometry.

- The lever pillar kind (action 8, axis 1, magnitude 768): **1,128 of 1,218** tiles have a hidden mover on their chain.
- Every other hinged kind: 0.

The 90 lever tiles without one end their chain at the tile itself. In the example (Tower of Caect'kern, N0000005 record 14), the tile has real geometry below it, so it is not bottomless and nothing changes for it. I did not convert all of them, so some of the 90 may still be bottomless and keep the 1,024-unit floor. That is the fallback behavior from before.

## Failed approaches

None. I considered a purely geometric rule (any mover under the tile), but following the tile's chain cannot pick up an unrelated mover from a lower room, so I used the chain.

## Notes on tooling

- On this lane the converter's default build folder (`..\..\..\DoomDaggerfall-build` from the tool folder) lands outside the allowed folders. I built with `-p:DungeonConverterBuildRoot=D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-build\trapfloor-converter\` and ran it with `dotnet DungeonConverter.dll`.

## Play-test list

1. Castle Lhishen: open a lever tile and step into the shaft beside the rising pillar. You should land about 97 units down, on a floor level with the pillar's base, instead of falling 1,024 units. Does it look right with Daggerfall's visuals? The floor is a collision floor with no Daggerfall surface drawn there, so you may see void below the pillar.
2. Direnni Tower: the same on the stacked-pillar tiles (records 36, 43, 55). The shafts are 192 to 384 units deep.
3. In any pillar dungeon, stand in the shaft while the pillar rises. The pillar should not push you through the floor or get stuck.
