# Remaining hinged trapdoors (20261123-212059)

## Result

The converter now treats every hinged trapdoor in Daggerfall the same way, from Daggerfall's data alone. The two-dungeon list is gone.

The sample covers every hinged kind and passes in all 14 dungeons. A first pass failed Direnni Tower, and the fix is the third part of the rule below.

Nobody has played this by hand.

## The rule

1. **Which tiles.** A model record is a hinged trapdoor if it is a TRP model, its action is 8, 18, or 20, and it lies flat, meaning its height is under a quarter of its shorter side. This is `IsTrapdoorTile` in `ActionExport.cs`. These tiles get one layer of collision at their real thickness. They swing without carrying the player, as before. Every hinged record in the game passes this test.
2. **Bottomless holes.** Some tiles cover a hole with no Daggerfall geometry beneath it at all. Doom needs a floor, so the converter had made the whole column solid, which buried the closed tile. Now, any 12-unit cell under a trapdoor with no surface below the tile's top gets an artificial floor 1,024 units down. This is `FloorBottomlessPits`. It needs no lists, and the converter logs how many cells it floored.
3. **Hidden movers stay hidden.** Doom moves a resting actor that sits below its sector's floor up onto that floor. The pillars and frames hidden under the lever tiles sit below the room floor, as they do in Daggerfall. Their collision cells were being lifted to 192 to 208, which made a 16-unit lip around the tiles and the 7.8-unit rest height in Direnni Tower. The previous task stopped this only for trapdoor tiles. Now no action collision cell moves except through `DFAction.Place`: `DFActionCell.Tick` does nothing, in `action_objects.zs`.

## The census (`DungeonConverter --list-trapdoors`)

There are 2,927 TRP model records in 1,303 dungeons, in 23 kinds. Every one is a 96 x 96 x 6 flat tile, on model 54000 or 54001. A kind is a distinct combination of model, action, trigger, axis, magnitude, duration, tilt, and chain root.

- **18 hinged kinds, 2,586 records.** Action 8 has 2,582, and action 18 has 4. There is no action 20.
- **5 other kinds.** Action 1 sinking tiles (69), action 16 (2), and damage actions 23, 24, and 25 (90 each). These are not hinged and are unchanged.

The biggest hinged kind is action 8 on axis 1, turning 135 degrees from a lever: 1,218 tiles in 359 dungeons. This is not a drop. Each tile covers a shaft with no floor that holds a hidden pillar and a frame, both action 1 models on the same lever chain. The tile flips open and the pillar rises out of the shaft, lifting the player about 95 units.

## Test sample (`trapdoor_regress.py`)

The script runs the census and takes the example dungeon of every hinged kind. It adds the known hard cases (Mantellan Crux, Orsinium, Direnni Tower) and the 2 original dungeons. Then it checks every hinged tile in those dungeons, 6 tiles per launch. It fails if any hinged kind's example record goes untested.

Each tile must hold the player while closed. Walk-on tiles are measured as the player arrives, and the original 7 must still trigger by real walk-on. Every other tile is measured at rest. Then the tile must fire through its real trigger or chain root and finish without stalling. Any riddle on the chain is answered. Tiles whose chain has no trigger must stay shut, because nothing in Daggerfall Unity can fire them.

## Known gaps

1. **Coarse collision on action 1 to 7 movers.** These still use the 16-unit cell grid, which is wider than the model by up to 8 units. The cell-tick fix removed the visible lip and the rest-height failure. The 69 action 1 sinking TRP tiles are not hinged and are unchanged. Their walking surface may sit 16 units high, so their walk-on trigger may never fire (code reading, untested).
2. **Pit depth is a judgment call.** Bottomless holes get a floor 1,024 units down. Daggerfall has no floor there at all. See the question below.
3. Tiles a body can reach only by falling or climbing were not found in the sample. Every sampled tile had level floor beside it.
4. Monsters on trapdoors, and saving mid-swing, are still untested.

## Failed approaches

- The first full sample run judged every tile at the moment the player stepped on. Pillar tiles failed on the 16-unit frame bump, even though the player rests correctly. They are now judged at rest unless they are walk-on.
- I first read Castle Lhishen's tiles as buried in solid rock. A raw-geometry dump (`--debug-line`) showed a floor with a hole and nothing below. The action dump showed the hidden pillar chain.
