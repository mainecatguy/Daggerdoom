## The arithmetic that decides the throughput
The owner kept the approved material odds (5% Daedric per piece at level 30), the value-based sell-off (1%-50% a day;
nearly every Daedric piece sells at 1-3% a day, so it stays 30-100 days) and asked for "usually about one Daedric piece at
the best smith", reached by fewer arrivals. Mean Daedric pieces in stock = Daedric arrivals per day x mean stay. One piece
needs roughly one Daedric arrival every ~80 days, i.e. about **0.4 Daedric-capable pieces a day** at a level-30 smith
(Python model, `sim.py` in the run's scratchpad; reproduced in the real build below). Shops 5 delivered about 6 a day.

Consequence: at that throughput a top weapon smith holds far fewer goods than its stock target of 75; the target becomes
a ceiling. This follows directly from the three owner decisions; see Questions.

## Design as built
- **Material budget** (`DoomerfallShopLedger.MaterialBudget`): expected Daedric-capable pieces per building per day.
  0.4 at level 30, rising as sqrt(DaedricChance(30)/DaedricChance(level)) so a lower-level shop never holds more Daedric
  than a higher one, capped at 3/day (reached below about level 9). Each item uses budget by its kind: weapon 1 (arrows 0),
  armor 0.1 (Daggerfall's armor roll gives plate, the only armor taking a material, one time in ten). Other goods use none,
  so general stores, alchemists etc. are limited only by the stock target and rhythm. Because the budget counts expected
  Daedric rolls, an armor-leaning smith gets ten armor pieces per weapon it would have had, and Daedric arrivals stay the
  same whatever the lean. **When card 08 makes every armor piece roll the table, the armor weight should become 1.**
- **Breadth**: arcsine (U-shaped) in [0,1]. Non-favoured kinds weigh 0.03 (specialist) .. 1 (generalist).
- **Lean**: 1-5 favoured kinds (specialists fewer), picked from the shop type's classic table in proportion to its group
  chances, rarity <= quality.
- **Rhythm**: 50% trickle every 2/3/4 days (at most share x interval/7 items per delivery), 50% big delivery every 7 (60%),
  14 (25%) or 30 (15%) days on a fixed day, filling to the ceiling as far as the budget allows. Nobody delivers every 5-6
  days. Budget per delivery = budget/day x interval, so a monthly crate brings a month of weapons at once.
- **Suppliers**: two or three regulars share 85% of deliveries (each within a factor of 2 of the others); 15% go to a
  Pitman-Yor stick-breaking tail (discount 0.5, concentration 1). Draws are i.i.d. from the shop's fixed supplier
  weights (the de Finetti form of the Pitman-Yor process), seeded by (location, building, day), so no history is needed
  and every shelf agrees on the day's supplier. Each supplier favours one kind of its own (x3). Newcomers keep appearing;
  a newcomer does not become a regular over time (that would need the sequential form and stored history). Supplier
  names and visible labels are card E (not this card).
- **First visit**: a shelf never stocked simulates the last 120 days (was 1), so the first visit shows the shop's rhythm.
- Doom ammo stays daily on the first shelf (owner: restocks often), outside the target; horse and cart kept there.
