# Smooth dungeon collision: paired walk-through (continuation)

Goal: the one item left from `work/notes/20261126-225039.md`: a paired Daggerfall + Doom walk through Privateer's Hold's
diagonal halls and a converted 45-degree ramp.

## Plan

1. Install the play build from this branch to `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-loop\review\dungeonedges-paired` with
   `scripts/play/install.py` (private build folder `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-build\dungeonedges`).
2. New tool `tools/dungeon_converter/paired_walk.py` + `paired_walk.zs`: the launcher with `--minimized` under
   quiet_run.py, a real New Game (driver from `tools/new_game`, same as `tools/demo_combined/walk.py`), arrival in
   Privateer's Hold with Daggerfall in play, then runs in 16 headings from each test spot. Per-tic positions go to the
   console log; the script counts snags and floor steps. Synchronized autosaves at the first spot and at the end.
3. Run it on the new map and, swapping only `privateershold.wad`, on the old map (`review\dungeonedges\before`).
4. (After review) New tool `tools/dungeon_converter/floor_check.py`: collision floors from a Doom-only engine probe next
   to the visible mesh's floors (read from `DFMESH`), to check each spot and claim against what the player sees.

How the runs move: each tic the harness sets the player's horizontal speed to 16 (running) in the heading; the engine's
normal player move does the rest (wall slides, step-ups, slopes). God mode, notarget, and pass-through-actors keep
monsters out of the measure. This is test-only and lives in the harness pk3.

## Findings

**The paired walk works.** A real New Game in both games (character made through Daggerfall's screens by the
`tools/new_game` driver), arrival in Privateer's Hold with Daggerfall in play (`dd_sync_df_phase` 3), 64 runs, then
autosaves that both games committed. Command (the whole script on the private desktop, since the creation driver must
see Doom's window):

```
python D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall\scripts\overnight\quiet_run.py --timeout 480 --cwd <worktree> -- python tools/dungeon_converter/paired_walk.py
    D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-loop\review\dungeonedges-paired --out <review>\walk-after --inside-quiet [--wad <old privateershold.wad>]
```

The saves in each run's `df.log` / `uz.log`: `save 1` is the New Game autosave; `save 2` is the harness's save at the
first spot (Doom 700,860 = Daggerfall space `933.3,1146.7,256.0`); `save 3` is at the end of the walk (`Doom frozen at
tic 4682` new / `4650` old, `at 937.3,-520.1,1152.0`, which is Doom's 703.1,-389.9 x 4/3, the dungeon scale). Each ends
with `Doom committed the bundle` in `df.log`. Daggerfall's own `position` field is the world map position (the same in
every save, as expected inside a dungeon).

**How the floors were checked.** `floor_check.py` prints the engine's collision floor (sector, floor height, slope) and
every upward-facing triangle of the visible mesh above the same point. It agrees with known floors (700,860: collision
192, mesh 192; `check-known.txt`).

**The ramp named in the earlier notes is a steep stair.** `stair840-new.txt`: from 760 to 920 at y -96 the mesh is a
69.4-degree face, and collision is flat sectors stepping 32-64 units, within about 16 units of the face. Faces steeper
than 45 degrees stay stepped by design (first run, known gap). The player cannot move from 840,-96 itself.

**The converted 45-degree surfaces are corridor-side bevels.** `scan-40-46deg.txt` (every grid point whose collision
floor, and the floor 10 units around it, is one 40-46 degree plane, with room to stand): 489 points, 458 at 44.4 degrees
(the converter's capped 45), 29 at 45.0, 2 at 43.0, all in narrow strips along corridor sides. `scan-18-46deg-wide.txt`
(same, 24 units around): 1,176 points at 26.6 degrees and 14 at 44.4. So the wide ramps are 26.6 degrees, and the
45-degree surfaces are bevels.

The bevels are real visible surfaces, and the new map matches them:

- `bevel52-*.txt`, across 52,620: the mesh is a 45-degree strip from x 48 (z 960) up to x 32 (z 976); west of 32 there is
  no mesh (rock). New collision: a 44.4-degree plane at 964.1 / 968.0 / 971.9 / 975.8 at x 44 / 40 / 36 / 32, against
  mesh 964 / 968 / 972 / 976. Old collision: flat 968 from x -8 to 52, an 8-unit step up from the corridor's 960, and
  8 units above the visible floor at x 48-52.
- `bevel931-*.txt`, across 931,1490 (in y): mesh 386.6 / 390.7 / 394.8 / 398.9 at y 1490.6-1502.9. New collision 386.7 /
  390.8 / 394.8 / 398.8. Old collision flat 392.
- In both, the collision plane goes on past the visible edge into the rock (no mesh from x 32 west, or from y 1507 north),
  as the old flat sector did. The player's radius keeps it off most of that.

**The spot 2300,1700 used in the first version of these notes is not visible floor.** `ramp2300-*.txt`: no mesh
triangles anywhere along the path from 2300,1700 to 2164,1836. New collision is a 26.6-degree floor with a ceiling 96
units above it; old collision is flat 480 with a sloped ceiling. Both are collision filler outside the visible
dungeon, so the walk there showed nothing about ramps. I withdrew it, and the earlier claim that the new map "matches
the scanned plane" there. The difference between the maps in that hidden space is not explained. `scan-clusters-mesh.txt`
also shows 2350,1600, 2250,1850 and 550,-300 (26.6-degree collision, no mesh). I replaced the spot with 690,-300, a
26.6-degree ramp the mesh shows at the same height (822.0).

**Walk results** (`walk-after\summary.txt`, `walk-before\summary.txt`). 16 headings x 60 tics at 16 units a tic from
each spot. A snag is a tic, between moving tics, that covers under half that run's typical tic. A floor step is a tic on
the ground whose rise or fall is over 6 units and over twice its neighbours' (a slope moves the same each tic and has
none; each stair riser gives one).

| Spot | What | Old map: distance / snags / floor steps | New map |
|---|---|---|---|
| 700,860 | diagonal corridor | 5,319 / 9 / 1 | 5,700 / 9 / 0 |
| 52,620 | 45-degree bevel | 3,021 / 2 / 7 (8-unit steps) | 3,135 / 0 / 0 (max 4.8 a tic, smooth climb) |
| 931,1490 | 45-degree bevel | 4,364 / 1 / 8 (8-unit steps) | 5,214 / 1 / 6 (6.6-unit steps) |
| 690,-300 | 26.6-degree ramp (unchanged) | 4,712 / 10 / 3 | 4,764 / 14 / 3 |

- **Bevels:** the clearest gain. At 52,620 the old map's 8-unit steps are gone: the player rises 5-7 units smoothly
  onto the bevel. At 931,1490 the 8-unit steps (headings 225-337) became 6.6-unit steps on the same headings, and its
  snag moved from heading 112.5 to 315. Heading 202.5 drops 20 units off a real ledge in both maps.
- **Diagonal corridor:** snags by heading, new: 22.5 (1), 45 (3), 112.5 (1), 202.5 (2), 225 (1), 270 (1). Old: 22.5
  (1), 45 (3), 112.5 (1), 202.5 (3), 225 (1). So 202.5 lost one and **270 gained one (new)**. Headings 270-337 stopped
  dead after 105-110 units in the old map and run 168-254 in the new one, sliding along the wall. On those headings the
  slide is slow (typical tic 1.1-2.3 at 292.5-337.5, 11.3 at 270). 22.5 and 45 go down the corridor's own stair (-179).
- **26.6-degree ramp at 690,-300:** this ramp was a plane before too, and the traces are nearly the same. Heading
  202.5 has 4 snags in the new map against 0 in the old (more on 22.5-67.5 in both maps, going down a long descent
  of -438). I did not trace the 202.5 snags to a converter pass.

## Failed approaches and snags

- The first launches stalled on Daggerfall's race screen: EnumWindows cannot see a window on another desktop, so the
  whole script must run inside quiet_run.py. Importing `run_new_game` also removes `--inside-quiet` from `sys.argv`, so
  the script asks that module for the flag.
- Without `-dfuitest 0` the creation clicks are not accepted. That flag also makes Doom screenshot every open menu (the
  `shots` folders); harmless.
- `level.MakeScreenShot()` from the harness left no files, so I removed it. There are no in-game pictures of the walk;
  the top-down before/after views are in `review\dungeonedges\screenshots` from the first run.
- The first choice of a 26.6-degree spot (2300,1700) came from the collision scan alone and turned out to be hidden
  filler. Every spot is now checked against the mesh.
