# Swimmable water (run 20261122-224806)

## Result

Converted dungeons now have swimmable water from each block's Daggerfall water level. No engine code changed.
Demo: `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-loop\review\swimmable-water\play.bat` (Direnni Tower, starts above open water).

## How it works

- Daggerfall stores a block's water height on its Start marker (flat 199.10): `SoundIndex * 8` native units, 0 for none. `Water.cs` reads the first marker in every block, as `DaggerfallDungeon.FindMarkers` does.
- Each walkable span in that block whose floor is below the line gets its own swimmable 3D floor, from that span's floor to the water line or its ceiling, whichever is lower.
- Because volumes are per span, the solid slab between stacked rooms stays dry and a room above the line gets no water. A room below the line in the same column floods independently.
- Water stops at the block edge, matching Daggerfall's per-block water.
- Water state is part of the sector signature, so shorelines split sectors only in wet dungeons.
- UZDoom's translucent pass still runs in mesh view, so the 3D floor's surface draws over the Daggerfall mesh with no engine change.
- The mesh draws without sector fog, so `water_view` adds a small underwater screen tint. Anything launching converted dungeons must load it to get the tint. Swimming works without it.

## Not verified

- A byte comparison of the dry map before and after. The session's permissions blocked comparing files outside the worktree.
- The full reachability check (`check_map.py`) on wet dungeons. It runs for minutes, over this run's 30-second launch limit. Water is non-solid, so collision should be unchanged, but shoreline sector splits were not walked.
- Sloped and door spans get water, but the probes skip them.
- Daggerfall's mesh could not be exported, so its water was checked by probes and swimming only, not by eye.

## Failed approaches

- Setting the jump button from an event handler or a thinker had no effect, because the engine runs player thinking first. The check uses a test-only player subclass instead.
- `+wait` on the command line does not delay later commands. Screenshots use the alias pattern from `compare.py`.

## Known gaps

- Monsters walk along the bottom of flooded rooms. They do not swim.
- Swimming skill, drowning, and water breathing wait for the engine sync, as the goal states.
- The water surface uses Doom's `FWATER1` at partial opacity, not Daggerfall's water texture.

## Play-test list

1. Run `play.bat`. Does the surface read clearly as water from above and below?
2. Is the underwater tint the right strength?
3. Does swimming feel right, including sinking speed and speed while holding Jump?
4. Can you climb out of the water at a pool edge without getting stuck?
5. Find a room stacked over or under a flooded room. Is the dry room free of water visuals and swimming?
6. Press F1 and check that the collision view's water lines up with the mesh.
7. Do shots, splashes, and monsters look acceptable around water?
