# Smooth dungeon collision: diagonals and ramps (run 20261126-225039)

Goal: in Privateer's Hold, angled halls get straight diagonal collision edges and sloped floors become ramps, while the
sample dungeons still load and stay navigable.

Demo: `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-loop\review\dungeonedges\play.bat` (new map) and `play_before.bat` (old map). Before/after
top-down views are in its `screenshots` folder, old on the left and new on the right.

## Plan, as it went

1. Measure where the existing angled pass (`AngledCollision.cs`, run e9974648) still leaves jagged edges and steps.
   Wrote `tools/dungeon_converter/sector_view.py`, a top-down picture of a converted WAD's collision lines.
2. Fix ramps. Found the cause in the data (below), fixed it.
3. Fix the remaining jagged diagonals. First guess (fallback vertices) was wrong; the real cause was sliver sectors.
4. Check in UZDoom: movement parity flood, bullets, the engine reach walk on four dungeons, a demo load.

## What I found

- **Ramps.** The converter only wrote real slope planes for floors up to 41 degrees. Privateer's Hold's ramps are
  mostly exactly 45 degrees (normal z 0.7071; 1,595 floor spans). Doom's walking limit (`STEEPSLOPE`, 46342/65536 =
  0.70712) rejects a plane that is 0.00002 steeper than 45 degrees, so these became 12-unit grid steps. Steeper faces:
  686 spans near 60 degrees and 398 near 69 degrees (steps of 21 and 31 units per cell).
- **Jagged diagonals.** Within boundary chains, only 46 staircase corners were left. The jaggedness came from about
  1,500 one- and two-cell "sliver" sectors along diagonal walls, slope feet, and sloped ceilings. Each sliver has
  slightly different heights where the diagonal cuts the cell. One side of a sliver snaps to the real edge and the other
  cannot, which leaves a sawtooth (visible in `stacked_diagonals` and `center_east`). An example is the diagonal
  corridor beside the entrance: its walls have a 54-degree foot, and each cell over the foot became a 32-unit-high pocket.

## What changed (converter only, no engine change)

All in `tools/dungeon_converter/AngledCollision.cs`, with a one-line hook in `Program.cs`.

- **45-degree ramps become real slopes.** Floors from 45.6 degrees (normal 0.70) up to the old limit now get a plane.
  Planes are written no steeper than 44.3 degrees (normal 0.715), so Doom's stock walking rule accepts them. A 45-degree
  face is turned about its own middle to 44.3 degrees: its ends sit at most about 2% of the ramp's rise off the visible
  ramp (about 4 units on a 384-unit ramp), and the small step this leaves at the top or bottom is far under the 24-unit
  step height. Movement physics are unchanged. 1,595 more spans are sloped (3,552 to 5,147).
- **Diagonal slivers merge into their neighbour** (`AbsorbSlivers`). A one- or two-cell sector takes the layout of the
  neighbour it shares the most boundary with, only if:
  - a non-axis real edge (wall or slope crease) crosses the cell at the heights where the two layouts differ,
  - every floor matches within 4 units (stairs keep their treads; I first tried 24 and it smeared stair treads),
  - every ceiling is within 24 units,
  - door and water spans match.
  A sliver's extra gap may close if it is lower than a crouching body, or lower than a standing body and over a floor too
  steep to walk (the wall-foot pockets). Crawl spaces stay. 495 slivers merge in Privateer's Hold.
- **Smoothed staircase fallback.** When a fitted boundary conflicts, vertices used to drop straight back to the grid
  corner. They now drop first to a smoothed staircase midline ((previous + 2 x corner + next) / 4, only where both steps
  are at most 2 cells), then to the grid only if that still conflicts. All 552 chains that left their real edges now
  settle on the smoothed line; none returned to the raw grid (before: 730 on the grid).
- **Diagnostics.** `--debug-chains x0,y0,x1,y1` prints the boundary chains in a box with their supporting edges.
  `--plain-angled` builds the previous angled pass, for comparison. The stats line now reports slivers, steep spans,
  and staircase corners left. `parity.py` and `reach_all.py` accept `--converter-arg` to pass converter flags.
- `--grid-collision` output is unchanged (both new passes are skipped).

## Failed approaches

- The staircase fallback alone did not change the picture: the jaggies were not in fallback chains.
- Sliver merge with a 24-unit floor tolerance merged stair treads into each other. The floor tolerance is now 4.
- Closing every sliver gap under standing height cut a crouch route. Gaps now close only under crouch height, or over a
  floor too steep to walk.

## Known gaps

- **Ramps steeper than 45 degrees stay stepped** (about 1,100 spans near 60 and 69 degrees). A real plane there would
  make Doom's player slide back, since its walking limit is 45 degrees, and the 60-degree steps (21 units) are
  climbable today. Making them slopes needs a movement rule change (the actor's `MaxSlopeSteepness`), which is the
  owner's call (see Questions). The 69-degree steps (31 units) are above Doom's 24-unit step, so they need climbing or
  a jump today; that is unchanged.
- About 900 small sectors remain. Many are real (posts, steps, door edges); some are slivers whose layouts differ by
  more than the tolerances.
- Big curved stair areas still have a stepped outline where the treads themselves are curved.
- Only Privateer's Hold was checked visually. The other three dungeons were only walked by the engine.
