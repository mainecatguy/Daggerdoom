### Town look-back: NOT MET (near flats match; far wilderness missing, blocked on the owner)

The owner asked that looking back from a town exterior still shows the wilderness objects. That holds only on the
town map's own ground. Beyond it, most of the wilderness the overworld shows is gone. This goal criterion is unmet.

`python tools/nature/town_lookback.py` (new). On the overworld, it stands 18 tiles west of the City of Daggerfall (group
9183, look point tile 26489.5, 36672.5), facing west, and records every nature flat within 20 tiles relative to the
player. Then it steps into the entry band, waits for the handoff to the town map `DDT09183` (converted at run time),
goes back to the same world point using the handoff's own translation, and records again.

| Radius | Overworld | Town map | Same sprite, within 1 unit | Missing on town map | Extra on town map | Worst height difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 tiles | 6 | 6 | 6 | 0 | 0 | 0.00 |
| 6 tiles | 12 | 12 | 12 | 0 | 0 | 0.01 |
| 10 tiles | 58 | 47 | 47 | 11 | 0 | 0.01 |
| 20 tiles | 256 | 131 | 131 | 125 | 0 | 0.01 |

Every flat the town map shows is the overworld's flat at the same spot, with the same sprite and height. The flats
"missing on the town map" all lie beyond the town map's ground, which ends 24 tiles from the town (6 tiles west of the
look point). The town map has no ground there to stand them on. Screenshot pair:
`review\nature-flats\screenshots\town-lookback.png`: the near trees are the same, and the far forest is missing on the
town map. Log: `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-build\nature-tests\lookback\log-lookback.txt`.

Cause: `SpawnTownNature` (`dd_treadmill.cpp`, the `town->Near(gx, gy, town->margin)` test in `SpawnNature`) spawns
only flats on the town map's ground, which ends `margin` = 24 tiles from the town. Entry is at 12 tiles, so right after
the handoff only about 12 tiles of wilderness behind the player keep their objects. The test cannot simply be widened:
the town map has no floor past its margin, so flats there would float against the sky. Showing the far wilderness
needs far ground on town and dungeon exterior maps, out to the overworld's view distance (64 tiles). That means either
much larger generated town maps (longer conversion, and the coordinate limits the town-stitch work hit:
`work/notes/town-stitch.md`), or drawing the overworld terrain and its flats around the town map as scenery the player
cannot walk on. Either way it changes the town map system, which this goal may not do without the owner. See
"Questions for the owner".

Dungeon exterior, same test: Ruins of Gaerhart Manor (group 6187, rect x 30902-30921, y 46646-46665), look point 18
tiles west, `--start "30875 46655" --look 30884.5,46655.5 --entry 30893.5,46655.5`, map `DDT06187`. Within 6 tiles:
36 of 36 flats are the same. Within 20 tiles: the town map shows 209, all of them the overworld's (same sprite, within
1 unit, height within 0.01), and 130 more lie beyond its ground. Log and shots:
`D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-build\nature-tests\lookback-dungeon`.

### Frame cost in dense woodland

`python tools/nature/frame_cost.py` (new). Four sessions at 1280x720, flats on, off, on, off (`+dd_natureflats`).
Each warps to woodland pixel 122,257, stands still facing four ways for 3 s each, then runs east for 15 s across a
chunk shift. Frame times come from `RenderOverlay`. The window held **4,349 to 4,390 flats** (not the ~14,000 the first
run guessed). The machine was loaded by other lanes, so the medians drift between sessions (flats-off ranged from 11.2
to 17.2 ms standing).

| | Flats on | Flats off |
|---|---|---|
| Standing, median frame | 16.7 ms (17.3, 16.2) | 13.0 ms (17.2, 11.2) |
| Standing, p95 | 24.9 ms | 23.8 ms |
| Running, median frame | 22.4 ms (23.5, 21.2) | 15.4 ms (15.7, 15.0) |
| Running, p99 | 77.7 ms | 57.5 ms |
| Worst frame (chunk shift) | 237 ms | 227 ms |

Session pairs (on vs the off session after it) give the cost the data show: standing **0.1 ms** (17.3 vs 17.2) and
**5.0 ms** (16.2 vs 11.2); running **7.8 ms** (23.5 vs 15.7) and **6.2 ms** (21.2 vs 15.0). So about **0.1 to 7.8 ms
per frame** on this loaded machine, most clearly while moving. The shift hitch is the
terrain's, with or without flats. Per tic the flats cost little: `NOINTERACTION` actors take the short path in
`AActor::Tick`. The frame cost is the hardware renderer handling about 4,400 sprites in an open map where every sector
is visible. **Performance risk:** on a slower CPU this could be a large share of the frame. If it matters, a fix that
keeps the look would be to batch far flats into one mesh, as Daggerfall Unity's billboard batch does, using the
existing `hw_dfmesh` path. Distance culling would also work but would change the look. I did neither. Logs:
`D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-build\nature-tests\framecost\log-framecost{0..3}.txt`.

### Kind and density against Daggerfall Unity

The old DFU desert shot (pixel 227,477) was black in every run (`dfu2`, `dfu4`, `dfu8`). The pixel is next to the
southern sea, so I did not look into why and used an inland desert pixel instead: **565,391** (climate 224, archive
503, found as the middle of a block of desert pixels in `DDNATURE`). DFU dump: `nature-tests\dfu9`; Doom: `doom7`.

Flats per map pixel, as Daggerfall Unity draws them (`drawn_*`, read from its billboard batch) and as Doom places them:

| Pixel | Climate | DFU drawn | Doom | Density correlation, 16-tile blocks |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 122,257 | woodland | 4,402 | 4,390 | 0.85 |
| 565,391 | desert (inland) | 1,128 | 1,098 | 0.40 |
| 227,477 | desert (coast) | 1,160 | 1,168 | 0.49 |

Density matches within 3%. Desert flats are spread evenly, so their block counts vary little, and the correlation
there is mostly noise. The sprite set is the same archive per climate: Doom uses DFU's own climate-to-archive rule and
all 31 records. Side by side (top DFU, bottom Doom, 1280x720): `review\nature-flats\screenshots\woodland.png`,
`desert.png` (both show sparse dead trees, rocks and small plants; the Daggerfall Unity half also shows a cactus, the
Doom half shows none clearly from this view) and `mountain.png` (pines). Doom's ground is green
everywhere, also in desert and mountains, and its sky is a forest sky. That is the terrain texture and sky, which are
outside this goal.
