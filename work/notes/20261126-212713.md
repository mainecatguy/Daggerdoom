# Overworld terrain objects (nature flats) as in Daggerfall

## Plan

1. Port Daggerfall Unity's `DefaultTerrainNature.LayoutNature` into the engine, with every input it reads:
   the tile types from `DefaultTerrainTexturing` (noise, beach line, marching squares), Unity's terrain steepness,
   `UnityEngine.Random`, `Unity.Mathematics.Random` and `Mathf.PerlinNoise`.
2. Feed it from the same heights the treadmill already uses (`RawHeight`, `BlendedPixel`), plus a new `DDNATURE` lump
   (climate per map pixel, nature billboard scales) from the converter's `--world` mode.
3. Spawn the flats as **client-side actors** (`DFNatureFlat`, `+CLIENTSIDE`): the engine never writes them to a save,
   and they are laid out again from the rule whenever their ground is shown. Overworld: per 32-tile chunk, as the town
   billboards are. Town and dungeon exterior maps: the ground inside the walkable margin at map entry and after a load.
4. Check the port against Daggerfall Unity's own output with a test-only dump (`-dfnaturedump`), and take side-by-side
   screenshots.

## Design calls (engineering, within the owner's instructions)

- **No stored state.** Layout is a pure function of the map pixel (same seed as DFU: `TerrainHelper.MakeTerrainKey`).
  Client-side actors are `OF_Transient`, so saves hold none. After a load they are laid out again.
- **Places DFU keeps clear stay clear:** the location rectangle widened by 4 tiles, water and beach, tiles that are not
  pure dirt, grass or stone (marching-squares transitions), and slopes over 50 degrees. Daggerfall Unity has no
  wilderness roads in its core terrain, so there is nothing more to clear.
- **Season:** the archive follows Daggerfall's season as last sent on the bridge (`DDTownCache::IsWinter`), checked
  when a chunk or town is laid out. A season change while standing in the overworld shows on newly laid out chunks.
- Same flats on every map that shows that ground: the town map lays out the same global tiles from the same rule.

## Findings

Checked against Daggerfall Unity's own values (`-dfnaturedump`, `compare.py unity`):

| Port | Result |
|---|---|
| `UnityEngine.Random.InitState` + `value` | MATCH |
| `Random.Range(1, 32)` | MATCH |
| `Random.Range(0f, 1f)` | Is `1 - value`, not `value`. Fixed in the engine to `hi - (hi - lo) * value` |
| `Unity.Mathematics.Random.CreateFromIndex(i).NextFloat` | MATCH |
| `Mathf.PerlinNoise` as Ken Perlin's improved noise with his permutation, `(n + 0.69) / 1.483` | **DIFFERS** (worst 0.61). Right at integer points only, so Unity's permutation or gradients differ. Axis swap also wrong. |

Consequence: the climate, archive, chances, location clearance, water/beach/slope rules and RNG stream are DFU's,
but the dirt/grass/stone pattern (from Perlin noise) is not DFU's exact pattern, so flat positions will not match
tile for tile, and since the RNG stream follows tile types, record choice per spot also differs. Density per climate
and elevation should still be close (same thresholds on noise with a similar 0-1 range). Getting exact positions
needs Unity's real Perlin; ~60 samples are in `D:\dev\DoomDaggerfall-build\nature-tests\dfu\unity.txt` to fit one.

Failed attempts:
- DFU dump's pixel finder found nothing (`HasLocation`/climate calls in the test); replaced by pixels on the command
  line, chosen from `DDNATURE`/`DDLOCS`: woodland 122,257; desert 227,477; mountain 564,3; City of Daggerfall 207,213.
- The DFU dump's terrain was "not loaded": the streaming world is idle without a started game. The rebuilt test turns
  the streaming world on before teleporting; **that rebuild finished but was not run**.
- `doom_shots.py`: console `wait` lines from `+exec` do not wait during startup, so every command (and `quit`) ran
  before the map loaded. Needs a ZScript harness like `tools/overworld/runtime_test.py` uses.
- The stock `DOOM.WAD -norun` exit code was not captured (quiet_run output piped). Not verified.

## Not verified

- Nothing has been seen in game: no screenshots on either side, no frame times, no town map check.
- Possible performance risk: a dense woodland pixel can hold up to ~14,000 flats in the 128-tile window, all actors;
  a shift moves ~3/4 of them. Unmeasured.
- Client-side actors being drawn by the hardware renderer is assumed, not seen.
